# Search-strategy scale review: benchmark, inflation diagnosis and v0.8 candidate (2026-10-05)

Role: search methodologist (methods advice to A and D). Executed by an AI agent. **No eligibility decision, no PRESS decision and no edit to the strategy or protocol files were made.** All PubMed runs below are parser-validation/diagnostic runs, not formal searches; they produce no PRISMA counts. Scratch files (every query string, UTC time, count, PMID set and title sample) are in `/tmp/strategy_review_2026/` (`query_log.jsonl`, `counts_*.json`, `ids_*.json`, `removed_samples.json`, `benchmark_A.json`, `benchmark_B.json`, `projections.json`, `v08_counts.json`). Privacy: User-Agent `scoping-review-search/1.0`, `tool=scoping-review-search`, no API key, no e-mail in any request; E-utilities requests spaced at least 1 s apart per process (at most three processes at once).

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Benchmark (23 reviews, 2018-2026; 22 with flow counts).** Records identified from databases: median **2,360** (IQR 1,150-7,580; range 105-23,814; 90th percentile about 11,100). Narrow single-marker/family/single-exposure SRs (n = 14): median 1,815 (IQR 1,150-7,890; max 23,814, Walzik 2024). Broad multi-family SRs and scoping reviews (n = 8): median 4,690 (IQR 2,025-7,140; max 10,976, Hayes 2023). Records screened after deduplication (n = 20): median 1,396, max 15,418. Typical database set: PubMed/MEDLINE (23/23) + Web of Science (19/23) + Scopus (14/23), with Embase (11/23) or SPORTDiscus (10/23); median 3 databases. Several used no date, language or human filter at the search stage (Walzik 2024, Trybulski 2026, Sardeli 2024, Haunhorst 2022, Hayes 2023), but none had a concept set as broad as ours.
2. **Where v0.7 sits.** The v0.7 projection of **33,000-48,000 unique records** (raw 113,000-124,000; the team's 60-90k raw figure is too low because WoS and Scopus EI+EO alone are about 65,000) is **2.2-3.1x the largest deduplicated benchmark (15,418)** and about 6-9x the median screened count of the broad scoping reviews (about 5,500). A is right that it is outside the published range.
3. **Diagnosis.** About 40% of the PubMed pool is removable noise of three kinds: **animal-only records** (4,360; 21.7%), **generic exercise words matched only in abstracts** (`train*`, `race*`, `aerobic`, `competition*`, `sport*`; 2,368; 11.8%), and **reviews, editorials, letters, comments and case reports** (2,954; 14.7%). Removing all three loses no positive seed (10/10 original, 44/44 expanded), no pilot ADVANCE record (10/10) and no eligible-looking title in a 20-title sample. The remaining volume is **a consequence of the deliberately broad PCC**, not a defect: in the 50-record pilot, 10/50 (20%; Wilson 95% CI 11-33%) were advanced at title/abstract, which is a high precision for a review search and implies about 2,300-6,600 PubMed records that would go to full text. No restriction tested could cut much further without losing positive seeds or pilot-advanced records (R1 human-only MeSH, R4, R5, `exercise-induced`).
4. **Recommendation: v0.8 = v0.7 + Cochrane-style animal-only exclusion (R1b) + generic E terms moved to title (R2) + publication-type exclusion (R6).** PubMed **11,448** (-43.0%; EI 10,097, EO 2,082); positive seeds **54/54** that v0.7 retrieves (10/10 original + 44/44 expanded); boundary 7/7; pilot ADVANCE 10/10. Projected five-database unique total **about 20,000-31,000** (raw about 71,000-83,000). If A also adopts the seed-expansion T repair (Package B, `seed_expansion_2026-10-05.md` §5), do it in the same revision: PubMed **11,809**, positive seeds 60/61 (only X50 missed), projected unique 20,000-32,000. Even after v0.8 the pool is 1.3-2x the largest benchmark. Further reduction needs a **scope (PCC) decision by A**, not a search fix.
5. **Gates.** R1b and R6 move FT02/FT01 rules into the query. That contradicts protocol E ("all eligibility rules are applied at screening rather than in the query") and strategy §1 ("Do not add ... human ... filter"). It therefore needs a new amendment (next free ID; PRE-004/PRE-005 are already staged for v3.1) in the v3.1 release, plus **A's PRESS re-sign-off** (the v0.7 sign-off is void for v0.8; R2 also touches PRESS condition 9.4b on `train*`). The PubMed formal export (`formal_runs/2026-10-05/pubmed/` is empty) and D's manual exports (WoS EO 1-500 is already exported under v0.7) must **wait** for v0.8, PRESS and v3.1.

**Database sets.** Every review searched PubMed/MEDLINE (23/23). The others were Web of Science (19), Scopus (14), Embase (11), SPORTDiscus (10), Cochrane (7), CINAHL (2) and Google Scholar (2). The median was 3 databases; seven reviews used 5 or more. Our five-database set (PubMed, WoS, Scopus, Embase, SPORTDiscus) was used in full only by Hayes 2023 (scoping; 10,976 identified). ## 2. Part 2: diagnosis of the inflation (PubMed, live)

### 2.1 Method

- **Base.** Blocks were read byte-for-byte from strategy v0.7 §3. The union string hash `261e368940b5` is identical to Addendum 3 and to the pilot pool. Re-run at 2026-10-05T09:41:44Z: **20,090** records (EI 17,445; EO 4,004). The PMID set is identical to `04_screening/pilot_2026-10-05/pool_pmids.txt`.
- **Variants.** Each is a restriction of the base. R1a `AND humans[mh]`. R1b `NOT (animals[mh] NOT humans[mh])`. R2: `sport*`, `train*` (including the train* inside the resistance clause), `race*`, `competition*`, `endurance`, `aerobic` and the four `interval` phrases moved from `[tiab]` to `[ti]`; E MeSH unchanged. R3: T minus `"time course"`, `"time-course"`, `"pre and post"`, `"baseline and post"`, `"pre-exercise"`, `"exercise test*"`, `"exercise session*"` and `"exercise challenge*"`. R3e: T minus `"exercise-induced"` only. R3x: R3 plus R3e. R4: I minus `inflammat*`, `CRP`, `"C-reactive protein"`, `"C reactive protein"` and the C-Reactive Protein MeSH heading. R5: base AND (I or O free text as `[ti]`, or I/O MeSH as `[majr]`). R6 `NOT (review[pt] OR editorial[pt] OR letter[pt] OR comment[pt] OR "case reports"[pt])`. Combinations as named.
- **Per variant.** EI, EO and union counts were obtained with `esearch`. The full union PMID set was downloaded in publication-year partitions; each set's size equals its count. Everything else was computed locally against that set:
  - seed recall over all positive seeds with a PubMed record: 10 original (POS-A1-A3, S1, S3, C1-C5) plus the 51 seeds X01-X51 of `seed_expansion_2026-10-05.md`, of which v0.7 retrieves 44. Boundary seeds (7) and CIT seeds are listed too. The expansion file appeared at 10:10Z, after this work began at 09:40Z. It was used because every variant is a subset of the base, so recall can be computed exactly from the stored PMID sets;
  - the 50 pilot TA records (10 ADVANCE, 40 EXCLUDE_TA);
  - the 273 backward-citation PMIDs from the five open-window reviews that lie in the pool (`citation_chasing_round1_2026-10-05/union_dedup.csv`, in_pool = yes). These serve as a topic-relevance proxy and are mostly primary exercise-immunology studies;
  - a random sample of 20 records each variant removes (`random.Random(20261005)` over the sorted removed PMIDs; titles from `esummary`).
- **Title-sample caveat.** The 20-title checks are a **rough check by one AI reader**. Doubtful titles were checked against the abstract, but this is not screening and not an eligibility decision. A 0/20 result is compatible with up to about 14% eligible in the removed set (one-sided 95%). It is a noise indicator, not a recall estimate.

## 4. Part 3: recommendation

### 4.1 Judgement

The scale is **mainly a consequence of the deliberately broad PCC**: any identifiable bout, all immune cells, functions and mediators plus an omics route, adults, no date or language limit, five databases. The PubMed pool has a 20% title/abstract advance rate in the pilot, so it is not dominated by noise. About 40% of it is removable noise, however (animal-only, abstract-only generic exercise words, non-primary publication types), and removing that is a legitimate correction, not a recall sacrifice. The benchmark supports A's intuition that 35-50k unique records is far outside the published range: the largest published review screened 15,418. It also shows that 10-24k identified records occur in this field even for narrower questions. After v0.8 (about 20-31k unique) the review would still sit above every benchmark. Getting into the benchmark range would need a **scope decision by A** (for example narrowing marker families or the omics route, or prioritising a date range). The search cannot achieve that without losing positive seeds: every stronger lever tested (R1a, R4, R5, `exercise-induced`) did.
