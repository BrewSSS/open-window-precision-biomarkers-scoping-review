## 1. Search logic and boundaries

Main query on every platform from v0.7: **E AND (I OR O) AND T** (team decision of A, 2026-10-04; contract amendment PRE-003). Run it as two routes in each database and combine their exports after download and deduplication:

- Route EI: **E AND I AND T** (exercise/exertion AND direct immune cells, function or recognised immune mediators AND post-exercise/acute-bout wording).
- Route EO: **E AND O AND T** (exercise/exertion AND omics/molecular-profiling terms AND post-exercise/acute-bout wording).
- Union: **E AND (I OR O) AND T**. T is the post-exercise/acute-bout block in §8: free-text wording for an exercise bout, the post-exercise or recovery period, pre/post sampling and a few "<time unit> after/post" constructions, plus, where a thesaurus offers one, a post-exercise heading (PubMed: Post-Exercise Recovery, D000096062, exploded). T is a concept block, not a timing limit: it contains no numeric cut-off and does not encode the 0-72 h window. Do not add an age, sex, training-status, health, human, language, publication-date, peer-review, biomarker, precision, infection-risk or "open window" filter. Apply the population, design, timing and immune-link rules in human screening. Reviews and animal studies may appear in searches but are not core eligible original human reports; reviews support citation chasing only. Preprints are sought in a separately logged supplemental search (with the same T block) and kept out of peer-reviewed core counts.

**Why T is required: the recall-versus-feasibility trade-off.** This change trades some recall for feasibility, and the size of the recall loss is not known.
- *Volume.* Without T, the v0.6 union retrieves 255,019 PubMed records (parser-validation count, 2026-10-04). Dual title/abstract screening at 1 min per record per reviewer would take about 8,501 person-hours for PubMed alone, or roughly 12,751-21,252 hours across the five databases before deduplication (assumed 1.5-2.5 times the PubMed count). Adding the Addendum-2 T block (V3) reduced PubMed to 15,883 records. The v0.7 T block gives 20,090 PubMed records (R1 17,445; R2 4,004), about 670 person-hours for PubMed and 1,005-1,674 hours across five databases raw under the same assumption (`platform_validation_2026-10-04.md`, Addendum 3).
- *Seed recall.* In PubMed v0.7 retrieves 10/10 positive seeds and 7/7 boundary seeds; 19/24 seeds overall. The five misses are the two expected exclusions NEG-E1 (resting cross-sectional) and NEG-E2 (animal), and three reviews (CIT-R3, CIT-R4, CIT-R5) that enter through citation chasing anyway. None has any T wording. The 10/10 is not an independent recall test. Positive seed POS-C1 was lost by the Addendum-2 T because PubMed silently drops the phrase "after a marathon". The proximity repair (`"after marathon"[tiab:~1]`) and the phrases that also recover POS-C1 (`"after race"[tiab:~1]`, `"after strenuous exercise"[tiab:~0]`) were designed after that miss was seen. The seed set is small (10 PubMed positives), was chosen for marker families, and several seeds were chosen because they state their timing explicitly.
- *Known blind spot.* T cannot find a record whose title and abstract give the timing only as numbers (for example "blood was sampled at 1 h and 24 h") and contain none of the bout, post-exercise, recovery or pre/post wording. No current seed tests this case. The v0.7 numeric additions (`"min post"`, `"h after"`, `"hours post"`, with `"h post"` and `"hours after"` already in the base list) cover only the "<unit> after/post" constructions. Bare durations (`"24 h"`, `"48 h"`, `"72 h"`, `"days after"`) and `kinetics` were tested and left out: together they would add 7,411 PubMed records and no seed, and in random title samples of their unique records almost none concerned an exercise bout (Addendum 3). Recall of T on WoS, Scopus, Embase and SPORTDiscus is untested.
- *Mitigations.* (1) Before the formal search, the screening pilot expands the positive seed set to at least 30 independently identified positive seeds, at least 5 of which report timing only numerically in title/abstract. Every seed is tested against the T-required routes; any miss is diagnosed and T is revised, with a versioned change log. (2) Iterative backward and forward citation chasing (§9.7) of every included study and of the five open-window debate reviews (CIT-R1 to CIT-R5; `known_seed_test_list.md` §D). (3) The T-free diagnostic run **E AND (I OR O)** is kept as a sensitivity check. During the pilot a random sample (saved seed, logged size) of records retrieved by `E AND (I OR O) NOT T` is screened by the same two reviewers. Any eligible record found there is reported with its rate, and T is revised before the formal search (v3.1 path in §9.4).

**Search by construct (v3 contract §3 and ruling 8.3a).** The exercise-induced immune "open window" is the review's organising construct: it defines the post-exercise period, sampling time points and marker families to be charted. It is not searched as a phrase. The 2026-10-04 collision re-check found only 18 PubMed title/abstract records (2018 to 2026-10-04) that combine "open window"/"open-window" with exercise/athlete/sport and immune terms (`02_preliminary/collision_recheck_2026-10-04.md` §5 item 1 and §6 query a). Original studies rarely use the phrase. The construct is therefore captured by the concept blocks E AND (I OR O) AND T: the phrase itself is not searched, while T describes the acute bout and the post-exercise period in general words (PRE-003). The 0-72 h organising window, the adult population, the A/B design rules and the immune-link rule are all applied at screening, not in the query. "Open window"-type terms (`"open window"`, `"open-window"`, `immunodepress*`, `"immune suppression"`, `"exercise-induced immunosuppression"`) may be used only (a) as an optional OR supplement inside the I block, so that a route reads E AND (I OR OW) and is never narrowed, or (b) through citation chasing from open-window-framed reviews. They are never AND terms, filters or eligibility criteria. If the OR supplement is used, log it as its own search-history line and record its marginal yield during seed testing. Window-based charting has a published precedent in the "prespecified recovery windows" of Trybulski et al. 2026 (Int J Mol Sci; PMID 42278474; doi:10.3390/ijms27114950). That precedent concerns charting, not searching.

Immediate-only studies remain discoverable through T wording such as `"immediately after"`, `"post-race"` and `"post-exercise"` (POS-A2 and BND-I1 are retrieved in PubMed v0.7); during-only studies are screened out. There is no 30-min minimum. The 0-72 h post-cessation interval is an **organising window** applied at screening and charting. It is not a search limit and not an inclusion cap. Immediate (0 h) samples are kept. Studies whose only post-cessation samples fall later than 72 h stay eligible if they otherwise qualify; those samples are extracted and tagged "outside-window recovery". No new exclusion code exists for them.

Population, exposure characteristics and biology are intentionally not encoded as a search block. Screening determines adult age (>= 18 y; mixed-age cohorts only when an adult stratum is separable; < 18 y excluded under FT03); acute Core A vs repeated-bout Support B (retained as the cross-bout open-window monitoring layer) vs background; health, active infection, time since the identifiable bout and position relative to the organising window, and whether omics has a stated immune objective/identifiable immune candidates. Post-hoc pathway enrichment in general muscle/metabolic work alone is insufficient for main inclusion. Precision language, the phrase "open window", author stance on the open-window hypothesis, and clinical/functional validation are not eligibility requirements. Exercise intensity, duration and mode are recorded at extraction and used as evidence-map strata. They are never search terms that narrow retrieval and never eligibility thresholds (v3 contract 2.1); the E block's mode/intensity words (for example `HIIT`, `sprint*`, `marathon*`) are OR synonyms that widen retrieval only.

## 2. Draft concept vocabularies (to be calibrated)

**E — exercise/exertion/sport context:** `exercis*`, `physical activit*`, `physical exertion`, `sport*`, `training`, `train*`, `workout*`, `athlet*`, `competition*`, `exercise test*`, `exercise challenge*`, `physical effort`, `run`/`runn*`/`running`, `jog`/`jogg*`/`jogging`, `cycling`, `cyclist*`, `cycle ergomet*`, `ergomet*`, `bicycl*`, `marathon*`, `race*`, `resistance train*`, `strength training`, `endurance`, `aerobic`, `interval train*`, `interval exercis*`, `sprint interval*`, `high-intensity interval*`, `HIIT`, `HIIE`, `sprint*`, `weightlift*`, and `swim*`. v0.6 replaced the over-truncated `cycl*` (it also matched cyclophosphamide, cyclin, cyclic and cell cycle) and the bare word `interval` ("confidence interval") with these specific forms (PRESS 4.1/4.2); all 24 PubMed seeds were still retrieved (`platform_validation_2026-10-04.md`, Addendum 2). Keep `train*` in the draft (contract; it may retrieve otherwise unlabelled training studies); it may be noisy, as may `race*` and broad run/race roots. Known `train*` noise: "trained immunity" (innate-immune memory, unrelated to exercise training) satisfies E through `train*` and I through `immun*` at once; on 2026-10-04 all 1,896 PubMed records containing that phrase were inside the v0.6 union. They are removed at title/abstract screening, never by a NOT term. During seed/pilot review log each term’s marginal eligible yield and false-retrieval pattern before any documented change; `train*` is retained pending that test, and no deletion is an improvement without recall evidence.

**I — immune measure/link:** retain the existing broad cell/function vocabulary and explicitly add marker-name routes: `IgA`, `sIgA`, `SIgA`, `secretory IgA`, `secretory immunoglobulin A`, `immunoglobulin A`, `interleukin*`, `interleukin-6`, `IL-6`/`IL6`, `CRP`, `C-reactive protein`, `phagocyt*`, and `cytotoxic*`/`immunologic cytotoxicity`. v0.6 adds leukocyte-population terms (`white blood cell*`, `mononuclear cell*`, `PBMC*`, `granulocyt*`, `lymphopeni*`, `lymphocytopeni*`, `leukocytosis`) and cytokine names and abbreviations (`TNF*`, `tumor necrosis factor*`/`tumour necrosis factor*`, `IFN*`, `interferon*`, `IL-10`/`IL10`, `IL-1`/`IL1`/`IL-1beta`, `IL-8`/`IL8`) (PRESS 4.3/4.7). PubMed needs at least 4 characters before `*`, so its block lists `TNF`, `"TNF-alpha"`, `IFN` and `"IFN-gamma"` explicitly instead of `TNF*`/`IFN*`; PubMed normalises the Greek forms (TNF-α, IL-1β) to the same index terms. `lymphocytopeni*` and `leukocytosis` are already matched by `lymphocyt*`/`leukocyt*` and are listed for transparency. These permit studies whose titles/abstracts name only the measured immune marker. Infection/URTI wording is not an I synonym in this draft; how infection terms are handled is reserved for A (PRESS 1.1a). Adjust only after seed/pilot sensitivity review; do not add cortisol/CK/lactate as immune terms.

**O — omics/molecular profiling:** `omic*`, `proteom*`, `metabolom*`, `lipidom*`, `transcriptom*`, `epigenom*`, `epigenet*`, `DNA methylation`, `methylom*`, `multiomic*`, `multi-omic*`, `single cell`, `single-cell`, `scRNA-seq`, `RNA-seq`, `RNA sequencing`, `microRNA*`, `miRNA*`, `lncRNA*`, `circRNA*`, `mass spectrometry`, `protein profiling`, `metabolite profiling`, `high-throughput sequencing`, `microarray*`, `gene expression profil*`, `expression profil*` (v0.6 added epigenetics, DNA methylation, microarray and expression-profiling terms and truncated the ncRNA terms; PRESS 4.4-4.6). The O route is deliberately sensitive and may retrieve generic exercise omics; screen immune relevance and do not count post-hoc enrichment alone as an eligibility link.

**T — post-exercise/acute-bout wording (required from v0.7; PRE-003):** three groups, all title/abstract (plus keywords/subject fields where the platform's E/I/O lines use them). *Base list* (the Addendum-2 T list): `post-exercise`, `postexercise`, `post exercise`, `after exercise`, `following exercise`, `exercise-induced`, `acute exercise`, `acute bout`, `single bout`, `bout of`, `exercise bout*`, `post-race`, `post-marathon`, `recovery period`, `during recovery`, `immediately after`, `hours after`, `h post`, `post-match`, `post-competition`, `exercise session*`, `exercise challenge*`, `exercise test*`. *Phrase additions:* `after` within two words of `marathon*` and of `race`, `after racing`, `after strenuous exercise`, `after prolonged exercise`, `after intense exercise`, `following` within two words of `bout*`, `post-bout`, `exercise recovery`, `recovery from exercise`, `time course`, `time-course`, `pre- and post-exercise`, `pre-exercise`, `pre and post`, `baseline and post`. *Numeric-timing additions:* `min post`, `h after`, `hours post`. Tested and **not** included: `kinetics`, `days after`, `24 h`, `48 h`, `72 h` (§8). PubMed also uses the heading Post-Exercise Recovery (exploded). Platform syntax for proximity and stop-word phrases is in §8.

For each platform, enter the displayed E, I, O and T block contents as separate search-history lines (omit the `E_PLATFORM =` label), then combine E AND I AND T for Route EI and E AND O AND T for Route EO using that platform’s own advanced-search/history controls. Do not assume that another platform’s search-history reference syntax transfers. Save the platform-displayed combined query and its parsed translation. Export the EI and EO result sets separately before deduplication.

The E/I/O vocabularies above are free text. Controlled vocabulary is a required pre-run addition for databases with thesauri because older records may lack abstracts. **PubMed (v0.6):** E, I and O MeSH lines are now part of the PubMed draft (§3). Each of the 42 E/I/O headings, and Post-Exercise Recovery (D000096062), now the T heading (v0.7), was confirmed on 2026-10-04 in E-utilities db=mesh as exactly one descriptor whose preferred heading matches the string used (UIs in `platform_validation_2026-10-04.md`, Addendum 2). All are exploded (`[Mesh]`) except Mass Spectrometry, which is `[Mesh:NoExp]`: its explosion (GC-MS, LC-MS, tandem MS and similar) adds 2,973 analytical-method records to the union beyond the NoExp form (PRESS 3.2). The 11 v0.5 candidates are kept; those now redundant under an exploded parent stay to document marker intent. The heading set remains subject to A's PRESS sign-off. **Embase (Embase.com or Ovid) and SPORTDiscus:** candidate Emtree and SPORTDiscus-thesaurus lines are given in §6 and §7. They are marked **PENDING LIVE THESAURUS VALIDATION**: the concept labels there are lookup prompts, not verified preferred terms, and D confirms each preferred term, scope note, explosion choice and date at login before any line is used. Do not copy MeSH labels into Emtree or EBSCO. Document any concept with no suitable controlled heading. Exact preferred headings, scope notes, narrower terms and explode choices on those platforms are a formal-run gate. WoS and Scopus use indexed free-text fields here and have no thesaurus line in this strategy.

## 4. Web of Science Core Collection — Topic field baseline

## 8. Required post-exercise/acute-bout block (T) — v0.7

**Status.** T is required in every route on every platform and in the preprint strings (team decision of A, 2026-10-04; contract amendment PRE-003). It was diagnostic only up to v0.6. Its role, the recall trade-off and the mitigations are stated in §1. The T-free union E AND (I OR O) is kept only as the pilot sensitivity check (§1, mitigation 3; §9.4). Translate the field tags per platform as for E/I/O.

**Vocabulary** (§2 lists the three groups). Syntax per platform:
- *PubMed:* `[tiab]`, plus `"Post-Exercise Recovery"[Mesh]` (D000096062; `[Mesh]`, exploded by default). Seven phrases are written as proximity searches because PubMed drops them as "quoted phrase not found": `"after marathon"[tiab:~1]` (catches "after a/the marathon"), `"after race"[tiab:~1]`, `"following bout"[tiab:~1]`, `"after racing"`, `"after strenuous exercise"`, `"after prolonged exercise"` and `"after intense exercise"` as `[tiab:~0]`. `"baseline and post"[tiab:~1]` is also proximity: the quoted form returns 2 records. **Base-list repair:** `"during recovery"[tiab]` returns only 5 records (the stop word "during" keeps it out of the phrase index), so v0.7 uses `"during recovery"[tiab:~0]` (15,374 records alone). In the union this repair adds 167 records and no seed. `"time course"`/`"time-course"` and `"post-exercise"`/`"post exercise"` are normalised to the same index entry by PubMed and are listed for the other platforms.
- *Web of Science:* `TS=(...)` with `(after NEAR/2 marathon*)`, `(after NEAR/2 race)` and `(following NEAR/2 bout*)`.
- *Scopus:* `TITLE-ABS-KEY(...)` with `W/2` in the same three places.
- *Embase.com:* `:ti,ab,kw` on every term; proximity as `(after NEXT/2 marathon*):ti,ab,kw` and similar. Embase help (2026-10-04): n is the distance, and `NEAR/1` means adjacent, so `/2` allows one intervening word.
- *Embase on Ovid:* `.ti,ab,kf.` with `(after adj2 marathon$)`, `(after adj2 race)` and `(following adj2 bout$)`. In Ovid, adj2 allows one intervening word.
- *SPORTDiscus (EBSCOhost):* `TI`/`AB`/`SU` with `(after W2 marathon*)` and similar. Terms are lowercase, as for E/I/O.
- *Preprints (medRxiv/bioRxiv):* proximity is not supported, so the `after`/`following` forms are spelled out (`"after marathon"`, `"after a marathon"`, `"after the marathon"` and so on).

**Login checks (pending; D):** (a) the proximity distance semantics on WoS, Scopus and EBSCO, where `/2` was chosen so that one intervening word is allowed under either reading; (b) whether stop words inside quoted phrases (`"bout of"`, `"pre and post"`, `"baseline and post"`, `"during recovery"`, `"recovery from exercise"`) are kept, ignored or reported as dropped by each parser, because the same failure that broke `"after a marathon"` in PubMed can occur elsewhere; (c) hyphen handling for `"pre- and post-exercise"`, `"post-exercise"` and `"time-course"`; (d) whether Emtree and the SPORTDiscus thesaurus have a post-exercise/recovery heading, logged as a candidate only. Until these checks are logged, the non-PubMed T lines are syntax drafts.

**PubMed evidence (parser validation, 2026-10-04; Addendum 3).** Within the T-free v0.6 union V1 (255,019 records), cumulative counts were: base list plus MeSH 16,066 (18/24 seeds; positive seeds 9/10, POS-C1 missed); plus phrase additions 18,287 (19/24; 10/10); plus numeric additions 20,090 (19/24; 10/10). Records each group adds that no other T term retrieves: base 12,757; MeSH 9; phrase additions 2,130; numeric additions 1,803. Three phrase terms retrieve POS-C1 independently of each other: `"after marathon"[tiab:~1]`, `"after race"[tiab:~1]` and `"after strenuous exercise"[tiab:~0]`. The numeric additions recover no seed. They are kept because they complete the "<unit> after/post" construction already in the base list (`"hours after"`, `"h post"`). Their unique records sampled poorly: of 20 random titles, 4 of the `"min post"` records, 2 of the `"h after"` records and none of the `"hours post"` records concerned an exercise bout. **Excluded after testing** (no seed; the number of records each would add to v0.7): `kinetics` 2,197 (about 1 of 20 sampled titles concerned exercise, and none concerned post-exercise immunity); `"days after"` 1,908; `"24 h"` 2,358; `"48 h"` 898; `"72 h"` 537; all five together 7,411. In random title samples of their unique records, at most 1 of 20 concerned an exercise bout. Low-precision terms kept as briefed and flagged for the pilot: `"time course"`/`"time-course"` (677 unique; 0 of 20 sampled titles concerned an exercise bout), `"pre and post"` (987; about 2 of 20, mostly pre/post training-programme trials) and `"baseline and post"` (162; about 1 of 20). Title sampling was a rough check by one AI reader for D. It is not screening and not an eligibility decision.

**History.** v0.4 expanded a diagnostic T (`post-exercise`, `"after the race"`, `recovery` and others) that was never run as a main query. v0.6 tested the Addendum-2 T as `union AND T` (V3: 15,883 records; POS-C1 lost because `"after a marathon"[tiab]` was dropped as a quoted phrase not found). v0.7 makes T required with the vocabulary above. The window rule is unchanged: the 0-72 h organising window is applied at screening and charting and is not encoded in T.
