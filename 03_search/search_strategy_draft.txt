## 1. Search logic and boundaries

**Single route (v0.9): E AND I AND T** on every bibliographic database (decision of A, 2026-10-05). E = exercise/exertion context; I = immune cells, function or recognised immune mediators; T = post-exercise/acute-bout wording (§8; required since v0.7 under PRE-003). The EO route (E AND O AND T) of v0.4-v0.7 is **dropped**. An omics study is eligible only if it has an immune anchor, that is a stated immune objective or identifiable immune candidates (protocol FT07). The eligible part of the omics literature is therefore E AND O AND I AND T, which is a subset of E AND I AND T. In PubMed none of the 61 positive seeds (10 original, 51 expanded) was retrieved only by EO (`seed_expansion_2026-10-05.md` §4; `strategy_review_2026-10-05.md`). The O vocabulary (§2, `O_PUBMED` in §3) is kept as the **omics tagging vocabulary** for charting and diagnostics; it is not run as a route. Omics studies enter through I.

**Limits (v0.9; PRE-006).** PubMed: three NOT lines (§3): animal-only `NOT (animals[mh] NOT humans[mh])` (Cochrane-style; records indexed for both humans and animals, and unindexed records, are kept); publication type `NOT (review[pt] OR editorial[pt] OR letter[pt] OR comment[pt] OR "case reports"[pt])`; child-only `NOT ((infant[mh] OR child[mh] OR adolescent[mh]) NOT adult[mh])` (mixed-age records also indexed Adult are kept); then `AND english[la]` and `NOT preprint[pt]`. Web of Science: `NOT DT=(Review OR "Editorial Material" OR Letter OR Correction)` and `AND DT=(Article) AND LA=(English)` (records flagged "Article; Early Access" still match DT=Article; Proceedings Papers, Meeting Abstracts, Book Chapters and Data Papers are dropped, consistent with FT01). Scopus: `AND DOCTYPE(ar OR ip) AND LANGUAGE(english)` and `AND NOT DOCTYPE(re OR ed OR le OR no OR er OR sh OR cr)` (`ip` keeps articles in press). WoS has no species or age field, and the Scopus index-term animal exclusion is untested, so on those two platforms the animal-only and child-only rules stay entirely at screening. No date, sex, training-status, health, peer-review, biomarker, precision, infection-risk or "open window" filter is added.

**English and journal-article restriction (A, 2026-10-05; a further change beyond the four v0.9 decisions).** Decided after D's first WoS and Scopus runs. Records not in English, and non-article document types (conference papers, meeting abstracts, book chapters, data papers, preprints in PubMed), are no longer retrieved by the core route. This reverses the earlier "no language limit" rule; the protocol side (eligibility and limitations text) is being updated by the protocol owner, not in this file. Preprints remain covered by the separate supplemental preprint route (§9.6): POS-S3, a preprint record in PubMed, therefore drops out of the core PubMed route by design. Non-English records reached through citation chasing are logged and reported as a language limitation, not screened as core records, unless the protocol owner's text says otherwise.

**Eligibility rules that now partly sit in the query.** Up to v0.7 every eligibility rule was applied only at screening. From v0.9 three are applied **in part** by the query: **FT01** (ineligible source type: reviews, editorials, letters, comments, case reports; PubMed publication type, WoS/Scopus document type), **FT02** (animal-only; PubMed MeSH only) and **FT03** (child-only cohorts; PubMed MeSH only). "In part" because these limits act only on indexed records: in-process, publisher-supplied and other unindexed records pass and are screened, and the WoS/Scopus limits cover FT01 only. Screening still applies FT01-FT03 in full to every record that passes. This change, and the reduction to four sources, is governed by amendment **PRE-006**; the English and article-type restriction is recorded on the protocol side by the protocol owner; it replaces the v0.7 sentence "Do not add an age, ... human ... filter".

**Field rule (v0.9).** Ten generic E terms (`sport*`, `train*`, `competition*`, `race*`, `endurance`, `aerobic`, `"interval train*"`, `"interval exercis*"`, `"sprint interval*"`, `"high-intensity interval*"`) are searched in the **title only** on every platform; `resistance` AND `train*` keeps `train*` in the title. In I, bare `complement` and `cytotoxic*` are title-only, with specific phrases in title/abstract (§2). WoS and Scopus primary fields are narrowed to **TI/AB/AK** (Keywords Plus excluded) and **TITLE-ABS + AUTHKEY** (Scopus index terms excluded). The narrowing holds only if D's seed test passes: every indexed seed in the 10 original positive seeds and the 4 Package-B seeds must be retrieved; otherwise that database falls back to `TS=` / `TITLE-ABS-KEY` (§4, §5).

**Sources (v0.9).** Three bibliographic databases: **PubMed** (MEDLINE/PubMed), **Web of Science Core Collection** and **Scopus**; plus **Google Scholar** as a supplementary source (§6). **Embase and SPORTDiscus are withdrawn** (decision of A, 2026-10-05; licence and feasibility). The protocol discloses this as a limitation under PRE-006. Their v0.7 drafts are kept in Appendix B for the record. Preprints, citation chasing and the registry-overlap check continue as in §9.

**Why T is required: the recall-versus-feasibility trade-off** (v0.7 text, kept; v0.9 update at the end). This change trades some recall for feasibility, and the size of the recall loss is not known.
- *Volume.* Without T, the v0.6 union retrieves 255,019 PubMed records (parser-validation count, 2026-10-04). Dual title/abstract screening at 1 min per record per reviewer would take about 8,501 person-hours for PubMed alone, or roughly 12,751-21,252 hours across the five databases before deduplication (assumed 1.5-2.5 times the PubMed count). Adding the Addendum-2 T block (V3) reduced PubMed to 15,883 records. The v0.7 T block gives 20,090 PubMed records (R1 17,445; R2 4,004), about 670 person-hours for PubMed and 1,005-1,674 hours across five databases raw under the same assumption (`platform_validation_2026-10-04.md`, Addendum 3).
- *Seed recall.* In PubMed v0.7 retrieves 10/10 positive seeds and 7/7 boundary seeds; 19/24 seeds overall. The five misses are the two expected exclusions NEG-E1 (resting cross-sectional) and NEG-E2 (animal), and three reviews (CIT-R3, CIT-R4, CIT-R5) that enter through citation chasing anyway. None has any T wording. The 10/10 is not an independent recall test. Positive seed POS-C1 was lost by the Addendum-2 T because PubMed silently drops the phrase "after a marathon". The proximity repair (`"after marathon"[tiab:~1]`) and the phrases that also recover POS-C1 (`"after race"[tiab:~1]`, `"after strenuous exercise"[tiab:~0]`) were designed after that miss was seen. The seed set is small (10 PubMed positives), was chosen for marker families, and several seeds were chosen because they state their timing explicitly.
- *Known blind spot.* T cannot find a record whose title and abstract give the timing only as numbers (for example "blood was sampled at 1 h and 24 h") and contain none of the bout, post-exercise, recovery or pre/post wording. No current seed tests this case. The v0.7 numeric additions (`"min post"`, `"h after"`, `"hours post"`, with `"h post"` and `"hours after"` already in the base list) cover only the "<unit> after/post" constructions. Bare durations (`"24 h"`, `"48 h"`, `"72 h"`, `"days after"`) and `kinetics` were tested and left out: together they would add 7,411 PubMed records and no seed, and in random title samples of their unique records almost none concerned an exercise bout (Addendum 3). Recall of T on WoS, Scopus, Embase and SPORTDiscus is untested.
- *Mitigations.* (1) Before the formal search, the screening pilot expands the positive seed set to at least 30 independently identified positive seeds, at least 5 of which report timing only numerically in title/abstract. Every seed is tested against the T-required routes; any miss is diagnosed and T is revised, with a versioned change log. (2) Iterative backward and forward citation chasing (§9.7) of every included study and of the five open-window debate reviews (CIT-R1 to CIT-R5; `known_seed_test_list.md` §D). (3) The T-free diagnostic run **E AND (I OR O)** is kept as a sensitivity check. During the pilot a random sample (saved seed, logged size) of records retrieved by `E AND (I OR O) NOT T` is screened by the same two reviewers. Any eligible record found there is reported with its rate, and T is revised before the formal search (v3.1 path in §9.4).
- *v0.9 update.* PubMed volume is now 7,846 records for the single route with all limits (diagnostic run 2026-10-05T13:23:01Z; 8,013 before the English/preprint limits; v0.7 union 20,090; v0.8 candidate 11,448). Package B (§8) recovers six of the seven T misses found in the seed expansion; X50, which gives its timing only as bare labels ("pre, post, 4 h"), is still missed. Package B was designed after the misses were seen, so seed recall on X01-X51 is no longer an independent test. The T-free sample (mitigation 3) and the 50-record pilot remain the independent checks.

**Search by construct (v3 contract §3 and ruling 8.3a).** The exercise-induced immune "open window" is the review's organising construct: it defines the post-exercise period, sampling time points and marker families to be charted. It is not searched as a phrase. The 2026-10-04 collision re-check found only 18 PubMed title/abstract records (2018 to 2026-10-04) that combine "open window"/"open-window" with exercise/athlete/sport and immune terms (`02_preliminary/collision_recheck_2026-10-04.md` §5 item 1 and §6 query a). Original studies rarely use the phrase. The construct is therefore captured by the concept blocks E AND I AND T (v0.9; E AND (I OR O) AND T up to v0.7): the phrase itself is not searched, while T describes the acute bout and the post-exercise period in general words (PRE-003). The 0-72 h organising window, the A/B design rules and the immune-link rule are applied at screening, not in the query. The adult-population rule is applied at screening and, from v0.9, in part by the PubMed child-only NOT (FT03; PRE-006). "Open window"-type terms (`"open window"`, `"open-window"`, `immunodepress*`, `"immune suppression"`, `"exercise-induced immunosuppression"`) may be used only (a) as an optional OR supplement inside the I block, so that a route reads E AND (I OR OW) and is never narrowed, or (b) through citation chasing from open-window-framed reviews. They are never AND terms, filters or eligibility criteria. If the OR supplement is used, log it as its own search-history line and record its marginal yield during seed testing. Window-based charting has a published precedent in the "prespecified recovery windows" of Trybulski et al. 2026 (Int J Mol Sci; PMID 42278474; doi:10.3390/ijms27114950). That precedent concerns charting, not searching.

Immediate-only studies remain discoverable through T wording such as `"immediately after"`, `"post-race"` and `"post-exercise"` (POS-A2 and BND-I1 are retrieved in PubMed v0.7); during-only studies are screened out. There is no 30-min minimum. The 0-72 h post-cessation interval is an **organising window** applied at screening and charting. It is not a search limit and not an inclusion cap. Immediate (0 h) samples are kept. Studies whose only post-cessation samples fall later than 72 h stay eligible if they otherwise qualify; those samples are extracted and tagged "outside-window recovery". No new exclusion code exists for them.

Population, exposure characteristics and biology are intentionally not encoded as a search block (the v0.9 PubMed child-only NOT removes only records indexed exclusively as infant/child/adolescent; PRE-006). Screening determines adult age (>= 18 y; mixed-age cohorts only when an adult stratum is separable; < 18 y excluded under FT03); acute Core A vs repeated-bout Support B (retained as the cross-bout open-window monitoring layer) vs background; health, active infection, time since the identifiable bout and position relative to the organising window, and whether omics has a stated immune objective/identifiable immune candidates. Post-hoc pathway enrichment in general muscle/metabolic work alone is insufficient for main inclusion. Precision language, the phrase "open window", author stance on the open-window hypothesis, and clinical/functional validation are not eligibility requirements. Exercise intensity, duration and mode are recorded at extraction and used as evidence-map strata. They are never search terms that narrow retrieval and never eligibility thresholds (v3 contract 2.1); the E block's mode/intensity words (for example `HIIT`, `sprint*`, `marathon*`) are OR synonyms that widen retrieval only.

**How to run.** On each platform enter E, I and T as separate search-history lines, combine them as E AND I AND T, then apply that platform's limit line(s) with NOT, using the platform's own history controls (or paste the single-line file from `paste_ready_v0.9/`; both must give the same count). Save the platform-displayed query and its parsed translation. Export the single EI set.

**Controlled vocabulary.** PubMed E, I, O and T MeSH lines are byte-identical to v0.6/v0.7 (42 E/I/O headings and Post-Exercise Recovery, D000096062, each confirmed in db=mesh on 2026-10-04; all exploded except Mass Spectrometry `[Mesh:NoExp]`). The I MeSH line keeps every immune heading, so indexed records reach I whatever words their abstracts use; this partly offsets the narrower free-text I list. WoS and Scopus have no thesaurus line in this strategy. The Emtree and SPORTDiscus candidate lines are withdrawn with their databases (Appendix B).

## 6. Google Scholar supplementary search — DRAFT for A (not validated, not run)

Google Scholar (GS) is a supplementary source, not a fourth bibliographic database: it cannot reproduce a Boolean strategy, its ranking changes over time, and it searches full text. Platform constraints: a query of at most 256 characters (reported limit; D confirms at the run), no truncation (plural and spelling variants must be written out), no field tags beyond `allintitle:`, and `OR` between adjacent terms with implicit AND elsewhere. Strategy, to be approved by A:

| ID | String (characters) |
|---|---|
| GS-1 (immune cells and function) | `"post-exercise" OR "after exercise" OR "acute exercise" OR "exercise-induced" lymphocytes OR "NK cells" OR neutrophils OR monocytes OR leukocytes immune` (152) |
| GS-2 (mediators and mucosal markers) | `"post-exercise" OR "after exercise" OR "acute exercise" OR marathon cytokines OR interleukin OR "salivary IgA" OR immunoglobulin OR "C-reactive protein" immune` (159) |
| GS-3 (omics with an immune anchor) | `"post-exercise" OR "after exercise" OR "acute exercise" proteomics OR transcriptomics OR metabolomics OR "single-cell" OR epigenetic immune` (139) |

Procedure. (1) Run each string separately in a signed-out browser session with personalisation off, "Any time", sorted by relevance, citations and patents excluded. (2) Screen the **first 200 results** of each string (20 pages of 10), in rank order. Results not in English are not screened (v0.9 English restriction). (3) Record the exact string, the run date and time (UTC), the reported result count ("About N results"), the number of results actually screened and, for each result screened, its title, link and DOI or PMID where shown. (4) Deduplicate against the PubMed, WoS and Scopus pool. Records not already in the pool **enter the same title-stage screening** (stage 1, PRE-005) under the same rules and then the same title/abstract and full-text stages. Report GS records as "other sources" in the PRISMA-ScR flow, separately from the three databases. (5) No GS record is ever used to judge database recall. Open points for A: whether three strings are enough, whether `allintitle:` variants should be added, whether 200 per string is the right depth (a common convention is the first 200-300), and whether the omics string GS-3 is needed under the single-route logic. GS operator handling (implicit AND and the scope of `OR`) is to be checked by D on a test run before the formal run.

## 7. Embase and SPORTDiscus — WITHDRAWN (v0.9; PRE-006)

A decided on 2026-10-05 to drop Embase (Embase.com or Ovid) and SPORTDiscus (EBSCOhost) for licence and feasibility reasons. The protocol discloses this as a limitation under amendment PRE-006. Embase's main extra coverage is European pharmacology and conference abstracts, and SPORTDiscus covers sport-science journals that MEDLINE does not index. Part of this is offset by Scopus, which indexes Embase-sourced journals, and by WoS, which covers many sport-science journals, and by citation chasing; none of this has been measured. The v0.7 drafts (Embase.com, Embase on Ovid, SPORTDiscus free text and the thesaurus candidates, all PENDING LIVE THESAURUS VALIDATION) are kept verbatim in **Appendix B** so that the decision can be reversed without redrafting.
