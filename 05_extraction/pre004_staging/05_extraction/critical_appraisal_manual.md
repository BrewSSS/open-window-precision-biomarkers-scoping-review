# Critical appraisal manual

**Protocol:** v3.1 (amendment PRE-004) - 5 October 2026 (`01_protocol/v3_design_contract.txt`; `01_protocol/amendments.json` PRE-004); tool sources checked 2026-10-02  
**Purpose:** descriptive critical appraisal of eligible evidence for the scoping review. This manual does not create an eligibility gate, a clinical recommendation, a pooled quality score, or a certainty-of-evidence rating.

## 1. Principles

Appraise the trustworthiness and limits of each eligible report with a tool matched to its actual design. Apply the appraisal to the methods and results that answer this review's questions, not to whether the authors use the words *precision*, *biomarker*, *immune* or *open window*. The open window is the review's organising construct (0–72 h after cessation of an identifiable bout); appraisal neither tests whether it exists nor rewards or penalizes an author's stance on it. Do not apply RoB 2 to every design. Do not add item responses into a total, rank studies, weight the synthesis by a score, or exclude an otherwise eligible report because of appraisal findings. Do not use GRADE.

Report findings descriptively: which design features are supported, which sources of bias or uncertainty are plausible, and what the report does not let the team judge. “Not reported” is not evidence that a method failed or that an effect is absent. Keep appraisal uncertainty separate from the direction, size, and statistical significance of a biomarker result.

This appraisal has two distinct parts:

1. **Study-design appraisal:** use the relevant current JBI checklist, or the conditional prediction/diagnostic tool below. Preserve every item-level response and rationale. Summarize design-related concerns in prose.
2. **Precision and assay evidence profile:** chart the project-defined dimensions in Section 5. These dimensions organize evidence for this review; they are **not a validated scale, checklist, score, or readiness ladder**, and they do not replace the design tool. Report dimensions separately without adding them together. They feed only the validation-readiness map, defined once for this manual (contract 8.3e): **Validation-readiness map: a per-marker, domain-by-domain descriptive profile across the seven precision domains; not a score, ranking, grade or development ladder.** Every prohibition on readiness scores, rankings or ladders in this manual refers to this definition.

## 2. Select the tool from the design and question

First identify the design used for the particular analysis being appraised. If a report contains distinct analyses with genuinely different designs (for example, a randomized training comparison plus a separately analyzed cross-sectional cohort), create a separate appraisal record for each relevant analysis. Link all records to the report, study, and cohort identifiers; do not count one cohort as multiple independent samples.

| Design / analytic question | Primary tool | Use and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Parallel-group or randomized crossover exercise intervention | Current JBI randomized controlled trials checklist | For a crossover, retain the RCT checklist responses and add a concise design-specific note on sequence generation, period effects, washout/carryover, and paired analysis where reported. This note is not an extra scored instrument. |
| Non-randomized exercise intervention; single-group acute pre/post challenge; controlled before/after without random allocation | Current revised JBI quasi-experimental checklist | An acute bout with baseline and post-cessation sampling is ordinarily a one-group pretest/post-test design, not an RCT. Record the absence/presence of a comparator and the exact exercise-to-sampling times. |
| Observational exposure-defined cohort with longitudinal follow-up | Current revised JBI cohort checklist | Use when exercise exposure/group is observed rather than assigned and participants are followed over time. A study does not become a cohort merely because it has repeated biomarker measurements. |
| Analytical cross-sectional comparison/association | Current revised JBI analytical cross-sectional checklist | Use for the design if appraising a contextual or eligible analysis. Under v3 scope (contract 2.5, unchanged from v2), habitual-exercise-only cross-sectional comparisons such as CIMA are `background` rather than `A_core_acute` / `B_support_repeated_bouts` evidence; do not put them in the core appraisal denominator. |
| True case-control sampling by outcome status | Current JBI case-control checklist | Apply only when participants were sampled based on outcome status and compared on previous exposure/test. Do not label an acute exercise pre/post study case-control. |
| Diagnostic accuracy of an index assay against a stated reference standard | Current JBI diagnostic test accuracy (DTA) checklist | Use for a genuine DTA question: an index test evaluated against a reference standard for a defined target condition with accuracy data. A biomarker association, an exploratory ROC curve, or pathway enrichment alone does not make a study DTA. |
| Individual-level prediction-model development, updating, or validation for a defined future outcome | PROBAST+AI (2025) | PROBAST+AI's published scope covers diagnostic and prognostic prediction-model studies using regression or AI/ML. For this review, use it when a study actually develops, updates, or evaluates an individual-level model for a defined future outcome, including a future immune-response outcome. Apply development, evaluation, or both as appropriate, and distinguish apparent, internal, and external validation. A contemporaneous classifier separating exercise from control, a molecular discovery classifier, clustering, or an ordinary association is not a future-outcome prediction for this review and does not trigger PROBAST+AI. |

For this review, use JBI DTA when the analysis asks whether an index assay agrees with a reference standard for a defined diagnostic target. Use PROBAST+AI when the analysis develops, updates, or validates a model that predicts an individual future outcome. The published PROBAST+AI scope is not narrowed by this project choice: it still covers diagnostic and prognostic prediction-model studies generally. If one report contains distinct DTA and future-outcome model analyses, create separate appraisal records; do not mechanically apply both tools to one analysis.

For mixed or unclear designs, two reviewers independently state the design and rationale before appraisal. Resolve design classification through discussion; if unresolved, use the prespecified adjudicator and retain both initial judgments. Never choose a tool only because its questions seem to fit a desired conclusion.

## 4. Two-reviewer procedure and documentation

1. **Before formal appraisal:** both reviewers read this manual and the archived official form(s). Use the protocol's 10 purposively selected calibration reports to check design assignment, report/cohort linkage, timing, and evidence-location conventions. These reports are calibration/boundary cases, not an included-study count. Resolve interpretation questions and version the manual before moving to the formal set. Do not invent a kappa or pass threshold for appraisal; the separate screening pilot threshold does not govern appraisal.
2. **Independent assessment:** two human reviewers independently complete the same design-appropriate form for each core eligible study/report. Each records every checklist item, a response, a brief evidence-based rationale, and a locator (PDF page plus section/table/figure/supplement where possible). Review all available full text and supplements needed for the question. Record the report, study, and cohort IDs to avoid double-counting multiple papers or the same participants.
3. **Status coding:** preserve the instrument's own answer choices in its original response field. In the normalized status field, use `YES`, `NO`, `UNCLEAR`, or `NA`. Also record `NR` when a detail is not reported. Lack of reporting normally maps to `UNCLEAR` for the judgement plus `NR` for reporting status; it is not an inferred negative result. `NA` means the criterion truly does not apply to that design/analysis, with rationale. Use `UNCLEAR` when the paper has information but the evidence is genuinely insufficient or conflicting. Never silently convert `NR` or `UNCLEAR` into `NO`. The same decision order applies to the precision profile and to extraction (v3.1, PRE-004): first ask whether the item applies to the design (`NA`), then whether it is reported (`NR`), then whether the reported information is sufficient and consistent (`UNCLEAR` if not); never infer from platform conventions, typical practice or figure icons.
4. **Resolve differences:** compare independent records item by item. Discuss using the source text and the official guidance; retain each initial answer, rationale, and the consensus result. If discussion does not resolve the issue, the protocol-designated adjudicator decides; the adjudicator's identity is pending and must be named before formal review. Do not authorize the same reviewer to overwrite their co-reviewer's original record.
5. **Report:** summarize item/domain patterns and material limitations in prose and tables, with examples and locators. Report unresolved judgments explicitly. State the number of reports/cohorts appraised only after formal screening; do not put placeholder zeros in flow counts. Keep background reviews, animal studies, and out-of-scope cross-sectional reports outside the core appraisal denominator unless a separate contextual appraisal is explicitly planned and labeled. Background reports retained at full text (disposition `RETAIN_BACKGROUND`, scope `background`; v3.1) are charted at reduced depth by one extractor and one checker and receive neither a design appraisal nor a precision profile. Record `eligibility_stratum` with the shared scope labels `A_core_acute`, `B_support_repeated_bouts`, `background` or `uncertain` (contract 8.3g), or the appraisal-only routing labels `FRONTIER_PREPRINT` and `BOUNDARY_PILOT`; appraise each analysis once, and count a cohort that contributes to both A and B once.

## 5. Separate project precision/assay profile

For each eligible report × cohort × marker family (the unit of the seven `precision_validation` domain rows, v3.1; an analyte-level profile is added only for a domain in which the report gives analyte-specific evidence), describe each applicable dimension below from the report. These dimensions implement the review's operational definition of precision monitoring; they are not a validated measurement instrument. Give a short evidence statement and locator for each. Do not grade an entire study by the number of dimensions with evidence, imply the dimensions form a sequence, or add them into a readiness score (see the validation-readiness map definition in Section 1).

| Separate dimension | What to chart (when reported) | Do not infer |
|---|---|---|
| Analytical reliability | Assay and platform; matrix; pre-analytics; calibration; LOD/LOQ; technical repeats; within-run/between-run CV or ICC; batch control; assay failure/missingness; cell-composition adjustment. | A technically sophisticated omics platform is not automatically reliable; a reported p-value is not reliability. |
| Temporal validity | Exact sampling time from exercise cessation and baseline; exercise-to-blood lag; repeated recovery points; pre/post-training and detraining/retraining timing; marker or function persistence. **Window coverage:** which time bins within the 0–72 h organising window were sampled (`window_bins_covered`, using the `time_bin` IDs of `data_dictionary.json`) and whether outside-window recovery samples (>72 h) exist (`window_coverage`: `WITHIN_WINDOW_ONLY`, `WITHIN_AND_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`, `OUTSIDE_WINDOW_ONLY`, `NR`, `UNCLEAR`). | An immediate post-exercise change is not recovery; a nonsignificant value does not establish return to baseline; outside-window samples are charted, not discounted. |
| Immune specificity | Whole blood, PBMC, leukocytes, sorted immune subset, cell counts, cell deconvolution, biofluid or cfDNA; immune objective and directness of assay; cellular source and candidate pathway. | Post-hoc pathway annotation is not a direct immune-cell effect; cfDNA is not an intact cell-intrinsic methylome; muscle/other tissue omics are not immune-cell evidence. |
| Individualization | Same-person repeated baselines; within-person versus between-person variation; measurement error relative to response; response heterogeneity; prespecified subgroup; per-person model; individual-level performance. | A group mean, subgroup contrast, “responder” label based only on raw change, or AI/ML method alone does not show individualized utility. |
| Functional or clinical linkage | Separate concurrent molecular concordance, immune-cell number/subset, ex vivo stimulation, cytokine secretion, NK killing, antibody/vaccine response, pathogen-confirmed infection, symptom-defined outcome; timing and whether the same biomarker was linked to it. | Transcript or ligand–receptor inference is not functional validation; symptom reports are not pathogen-confirmed infection; association is not prediction or causal effect. |
| Independent validation and use | Whether validation used held-out people/cohorts/sites/time periods; whether feature selection was nested within training folds; external replication and population match; and whether a stated monitoring/decision use was actually tested (decision context, threshold or action, and implementation/decision-impact evidence when available). Keep proposed or author-stated use separate from use tested in data. | A random cell/sample split that places the same participant in train and test is not participant-independent validation. A proposed use or a performance statistic alone is not evidence that a decision was tested. |
| Feasibility | Sampling burden, invasiveness, turnaround, cost if available, specialized equipment, reproducibility across sites, and exercise/sampling setting. | Absence of cost or implementation data does not equal infeasible; performance in a lab is not clinical utility. |

For each dimension, record one of `EVIDENCE_REPORTED`, `NOT_REPORTED`, `UNCLEAR`, or `NOT_APPLICABLE` and a narrative. These status labels organize extraction only; they are not scores. They are a coarser reporting-level view of the canonical extraction `evidence_state` (`data_dictionary.json` `vocabulary_crosswalks.evidence_state`):

| Extraction `evidence_state` (canonical) | Appraisal `precision_profile_status` | Evidence-map `reporting_status` | Evidence-map `validation` codes (independent validation) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `evidence_present` | `EVIDENCE_REPORTED` | `reported` | `independent_site_or_cohort` (supports) |
| `measured_null` | `EVIDENCE_REPORTED` (note states the null result) | `reported` | `independent_site_or_cohort` (did not validate/replicate) |
| `not_demonstrated` | `EVIDENCE_REPORTED` (note states what was not demonstrated) | `reported` | `none`, `resampling_same_participants`, `participant_level_holdout_same_cohort` |
| `NR` | `NOT_REPORTED` | `NR` | `NR` |
| `NA` | `NOT_APPLICABLE` | `NA` | `NA` |
| `UNCLEAR` | `UNCLEAR` | `UNCLEAR` | `UNCLEAR` | Keep direct functional/clinical outcome data separate from mechanistic plausibility and from the JBI/PROBAST+AI design appraisal.

## 6. Synthesis and safeguards

Present appraisal findings by design and by outcome/assay context. Distinguish risk-of-bias concerns, reporting limitations, imprecision/small samples, indirectness to the target context, analytical validity, temporal sampling, and missing functional validation. Do not merge them into one “quality” label. Do not let a favorable appraisal convert an exploratory marker into a clinical recommendation; do not let a concern about reporting erase a measured result. The output is the per-marker validation-readiness map defined in Section 1, together with the development needs it exposes; it is not a determination of clinical readiness or return-to-training utility, and it does not adjudicate whether an open window exists.
