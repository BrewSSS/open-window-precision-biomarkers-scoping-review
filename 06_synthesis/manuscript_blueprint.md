# Manuscript blueprint

**Planned title:** *Candidate biomarkers for precision monitoring of post-exercise immunity: a scoping review of conventional and omics evidence*

**Review identity:** JBI scoping review using PCC; report with PRISMA-ScR and PRISMA-S. This blueprint is for the future review manuscript, not a protocol publication and not a results draft. Formal database searches, screening, charting, and synthesis have not been completed. Leave all results counts and findings blank until generated from project records.

## Target journal and article type

The target is *Frontiers in Immunology*. Current official journal article-type guidance includes scoping reviews within the **Systematic Review** category, and the current article-type page gives a standard review structure of Abstract, Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion. The category/options can vary by selected specialty section and the live submission dropdown; verify the selected section and current instructions at submission. Preserve the actual methodology as a scoping review using PCC, JBI methods, PRISMA-ScR, and PRISMA-S. Do not force PICO, an effect-review structure, meta-analysis, or causal claims merely because the portal label says “Systematic Review.” This is a target, not a prediction of editorial acceptance. ## Provisional structure

### Front matter

- Title and short running title, if required by the current portal.
- Author list, affiliations, corresponding author, and equal-contribution notes only after confirmed by the team.
- Abstract, target ≤350 words pending current submission-form verification. Cover background, objective/questions, eligibility and methods, evidence-source/charting approach, main mapped results, and conclusion. Do not insert placeholder numbers as if results exist.
- Keywords: candidate biomarkers; exercise; post-exercise immunity; precision monitoring; scoping review; multi-omics. Final list to align with journal indexing guidance.

### 1. Introduction

1. **Post-exercise immunity as a monitoring problem.** Explain the need to interpret immune-relevant changes after exercise. Treat “open window” as a debated framing, not a settled diagnosis of immunosuppression or infection susceptibility.
2. **Candidate markers and precision need.** Introduce conventional cellular/mediator measures and omics candidates; explain why timing, exercise context, matrix, cell redistribution, hydration, nutrition, and assay reliability affect interpretation.
3. **What precision means here.** Define the operational framework as appropriate interpretation for a specified person/subgroup, exercise context, sampling time, and intended immune-monitoring use. Clarify that group-average change, high-dimensional profiling, or model complexity alone does not confer individualized utility.
4. **Review rationale and questions.** State the evidence-map need without “first review” or “no collision” claims. Present RQ1–RQ4 from the current design contract and distinguish marker detection, immune function, clinical infection, and validated prediction.

### 2. Methods

Use concise subsections that can be audited against the final protocol and search files. Report deviations and dates explicitly.

2.1 **Design and protocol.** JBI scoping review; version 2.0 working design; PCC and PRISMA-ScR, with PRISMA-S for search reporting. State whether and when a registration was submitted only after verified; current OSF record is a draft and not submitted. Describe amendments with dates/reasons.

2.2 **Review questions and operational definitions.** Reproduce the RQs and multidimensional precision domains. Define evidence states for reported, NR, NA, unclear, and not retrieved/awaiting classification.

2.3 **Eligibility criteria.** Describe human population, baseline health and handling of mixed/metabolic-health cohorts; prespecified immune-link eligibility; source types; language/full-text handling; and exercise strata:

- **A, acute bout:** identifiable bout/competition, baseline or suitable comparator, and ≥1 post-cessation sample. No 30-minute minimum and no hard 72-hour ceiling; immediate-only results describe response, not recovery.
- **B, repeated-bout monitoring:** the same immune-related marker is measured in the same individuals after at least two identifiable bouts/competitions; each post-bout sample has a known or reliably recoverable exercise-to-sampling relation, with a baseline or appropriate comparator enabling interpretation. Keep B separate from A and record overlap.
- Background-only studies and excluded source types remain outside core denominators. Public preprints, if found in defined supplemental sources, are registered separately and do not enter peer-reviewed core counts.

2.4 **Information sources and search strategy.** Name the actually searched platforms, exact platform/interface for each database, dates, coverage, search strings, limits, and supplementary citation-chasing. Planned sources are PubMed/MEDLINE, Web of Science Core Collection, Scopus, Embase (platform to be finalized), and SPORTDiscus/EBSCOhost. Draft strategies remain unvalidated until platform testing and independent PRESS review. Explain the two search routes (exercise + immune terms; exercise + omics terms) and their union/deduplication. Do not require “precision,” “biomarker,” or “open window” terms. The 2026-10-02 public-web reconnaissance must be disclosed as preliminary work, not represented as formal five-database searching. The final search cutoff is the actual last search date, not the reconnaissance date.

2.5 **Selection process.** Two human reviewers independently screen titles/abstracts and full texts. Report pilot sampling seed, 50-record calibration, raw agreement and descriptive kappa, conceptual disagreements, and whether a repeat pilot was needed. Persistent conflicts follow the designated adjudication process after the adjudicator is named. Report one primary full-text exclusion reason while preserving other notes. Give actual report-selection flow counts only after screening.

2.6 **Data charting.** State the 10 purposive boundary/design reports are calibration cases, not an included-study count. Describe independent human extraction of eligibility, timing, n, effects/uncertainty, precision domains, function/clinical outcomes, validation, and appraisal; bibliographic metadata entry with second-person verification. Define `cohort_id`, `study_id`, `report_id`, and assay-level observation. Distinguish participants, arms, biospecimens, cells/preparations, assays, and technical repeats; retain same-participant pairing.

2.7 **Critical appraisal.** Name the prespecified design-appropriate tools used and the reporting date/version. Summarize domains descriptively; no aggregate quality grade, no GRADE, and no unstated appraisal-based eligibility threshold.

2.8 **Synthesis.** Describe cohort-first and report-second counting; A/B overlap; domain-specific denominators; NR/NA/UNCLEAR; handling of missingness, nulls, inconsistent findings, and exact sampling times. State display-only time bins and separate exercise timing from symptom timing. Explain the distinction between function co-measured and marker-function association, between pathway annotation and direct function, and between internal and independent validation. No meta-analysis, clinical ranking, or composite precision-readiness score.

2.9 **Frontier register.** Describe any preprints separately, with version/date checks, linked peer-reviewed versions, independent denominator, and the same substantive eligibility descriptors where a report is assessable. Do not include them in peer-reviewed core totals or imply private unpublished evidence is used.

### 3. Results

Populate only from locked search/screen/charting records. Recommended order:

3.1 **Selection of sources.** PRISMA-ScR flow diagram, with real database/other-source and full-text counts. Never substitute zero for “not yet available.”

3.2 **Characteristics of the evidence base.** Independent cohorts first; publication reports separately. Summarize population, design, exercise, A/B strata, matrices/platforms, and immune-link basis. Flag overlapping reports/cohorts and validation cohorts.

3.3 **RQ1 — Marker and platform landscape.** Conventional markers, immune-cell omics, biofluid omics with a stated immune objective, matrix, exercise/population context, and stated monitoring use. Describe breadth without ranking by assay or number of analytes.

3.4 **RQ2 — Timing, recovery, and confounding.** Show exact source-reported timing with display bins; compare cell, mediator, pathway, functional, and clinical layers. Map control of hydration, nutrition/fasting, training background, plasma-volume changes, sample handling, and other confounders. Do not interpret non-significance as recovery.

3.5 **RQ3 — Person-level evidence.** Summarize individual baselines, within-person variance, repeatability, subgroup analyses, response heterogeneity, model development, participant separation, independent validation, and whether a stated monitoring or decision use was actually tested. Distinguish group-average effects from reproducible individual differences.

3.6 **RQ4 — Function, clinical outcomes, and prediction.** Separate function measured in the same cohort from a tested marker-function association; separate pathway inference from direct functional assays; separate self-reported symptoms, clinician-defined syndrome, and pathogen-confirmed infection. Summarize predictive model performance, independent validation, and tested monitoring/decision use as separate states; do not infer tested use from a proposed application or imply deployment.

3.7 **Critical appraisal.** Domain-wise descriptive results and limitations by design; no aggregate grades.

3.8 **Frontier evidence.** A separate, clearly labeled preprint table/register and separate denominators. State version and publication status at the final update.

### 4. Discussion

4.1 **Principal mapped findings**, organized by RQ1–RQ4 and limited to the evidence actually charted.

4.2 **Post-exercise timing and immune compartments.** Discuss the distinction between marker kinetics, leukocyte redistribution, immune function, and clinical infection; explain why sampling timing and control conditions matter.

4.3 **Precision-monitoring implications.** Discuss the seven-domain evidence profile (analytical reliability, temporal validity, immune specificity, individualization, functional/clinical linkage, independent validation and tested use, feasibility). Identify which links are absent or NR; do not rank clinical candidates or claim readiness.

4.4 **Functional and infection linkage.** Explain what co-measurement can establish versus explicit association; assess whether functional assays, prospective follow-up, case definitions, and prediction validation support intended use.

4.5 **Research priorities.** Propose repeatable within-person sampling, defined exercise exposure and timing, cell-composition/volume/nutrition controls, fit-for-purpose assay reliability, prespecified subgroups, test-retest estimates, independent validation, and clinically meaningful outcome ascertainment where justified by the map.

4.6 **Strengths and limitations.** Discuss search coverage and platform variations, language/access limits, the narrowed acute/repeated-bout scope, study heterogeneity, missing reporting, design-appropriate appraisal, and the risk of overinterpreting high-dimensional or group-average evidence. Clearly disclose the preliminary web reconnaissance and any protocol amendments.

4.7 **Conclusion.** State what the map can and cannot support. Do not conclude that an open window is proven, that a marker predicts infection unless validated evidence supports it, or that the review establishes a clinical decision rule.
