### 1. Introduction

1. **The open window as an organising construct.** The “open window” has been used to describe the hours to days after strenuous or prolonged exercise during which immune changes have been proposed to coincide with reduced host protection (Peake et al. 2017; Simpson et al. 2020). Whether such a period exists remains debated: lymphocytopenia may reflect redistribution rather than loss of whole-body function, and plasma-volume change, circadian timing and sampling alter interpretation (Campbell & Turner 2018; Davison et al. 2025). State explicitly that this review does not adjudicate that debate and uses the open window solely as an organising construct for the post-exercise period, sampling time points and marker families, irrespective of whether original authors endorse, reject or omit the hypothesis.
2. **Rationale anchor.** Peake et al. (2017) concluded that, “except for salivary IgA, clear and consistent markers of this immunodepression remain elusive”; present this as the auditable starting point that the per-marker map revisits descriptively, marker family by marker family.
3. **Positioning against existing reviews (no “first review” or “no competing review” claim).** The debate on whether the open window exists is occupied by position papers and narrative reviews (Campbell & Turner 2018; Simpson et al. 2020; Reitzner & Brodin 2026), and this review does not join it. Narrative reviews have proposed marker agendas around the window: tissue-resident memory T cells and their circulating precursors (Xie et al. 2026, *Frontiers in Immunology*), workload-dependent recovery of conventional indices (Shi et al. 2025, *Frontiers in Immunology*), the immune system as a systems-level biosensor of health and performance (Reitzner & Brodin 2026) and molecular biomarkers of training responses with minimum sampling standards (Mănescu et al. 2026); methodological commentary has questioned the clinical relevance of ex vivo immune assays (Davison et al. 2025). Systematic reviews to date address single marker families or single populations (protocol refs 4–7: exercise-associated non-coding RNAs, B-cell-related immunity, TH1/TH2 markers in older adults, exercise immunometabolomics). Registered reviews nearest to this one differ in PCC: PROSPERO CRD42020186264 (salivary IgA and respiratory infection), PROSPERO CRD42022324139 (acute HIIT), and OSF osf.io/4hsdr (same JBI/PRISMA-ScR method stack, but athletes aged 10–17 y and conventional markers only; this review is adults only). The proposed distinction is the joint, time-resolved charting of conventional and omics candidates within one organising window against separately recorded precision domains.
4. **What precision means here.** Define precision monitoring as interpreting an immune-related measurement within the organising window for a specified person/subgroup, exercise context, sampling time and intended monitoring use. Clarify that group-average change, high-dimensional profiling, or model complexity alone does not confer individualized utility.
5. **Review rationale and questions.** Present RQ1–RQ4 from the v3 protocol (RQ2 includes the descriptive author open-window interpretation) and distinguish marker detection, immune function, clinical infection, and validated prediction. All new citations must be verified against PubMed/Crossref before use (contract 8.3k).

### 2. Methods

Use concise subsections that can be audited against the final protocol and search files. Report deviations and dates explicitly.

2.1 **Design and protocol.** JBI scoping review; PCC and PRISMA-ScR, with PRISMA-S for search reporting. The protocol (version 3.0) was archived after team freeze as a tagged GitHub Release of the public project repository, deposited automatically in Zenodo with a dated, immutable version DOI (and a concept DOI for the record series); insert the repository URL, release tag and version DOI from `01_protocol/project_settings.json` only after the release exists (“DOI assigned on release” until then). The protocol was never registered in any registry: PROSPERO does not accept scoping reviews and OSF Registries was not used. The release preceded the 50-record screening pilot, the 10-report charting pilot and formal searching; if a pilot changed a rule, cite the v3.1 re-release (new version DOI, same concept DOI) with its justification. Describe amendments with dates/reasons from `01_protocol/amendments.json`.

2.2 **Review questions and operational definitions.** Reproduce the RQs, the organising construct and window (0–72 h after cessation; outside-window recovery beyond 72 h), the seven precision domains and the validation-readiness map definition above. Define evidence states (`evidence_present`, `measured_null`, `not_demonstrated`, NR, NA, UNCLEAR) and not retrieved/awaiting classification.

2.3 **Eligibility criteria.** Describe the adult population (≥18 y; generally healthy people and athletes; older adults included and reported as a subgroup; mixed-age cohorts only with a separable adult stratum; <18 y excluded), baseline health and handling of mixed/metabolic-health cohorts; prespecified immune-link eligibility (cortisol, creatine kinase, lactate or HRV alone insufficient); source types; language/full-text handling; and exercise strata:

- **Core A (`A_core_acute`), acute bout:** identifiable bout/competition of any intensity, duration or mode (recorded as reported and used as map strata, never as eligibility thresholds), baseline or suitable comparator, and ≥1 post-cessation sample. No 30-minute minimum; 0–72 h is the organising window, not a ceiling, and later samples are charted as outside-window recovery. Immediate-only results describe response, not recovery.
- **Support B (`B_support_repeated_bouts`), repeated post-exercise windows:** the same immune-related marker is measured in the same individuals after at least two identifiable bouts/competitions; each post-bout sample has a known or reliably recoverable exercise-to-sampling relation, with a baseline or appropriate comparator enabling interpretation. Retained as the cross-bout monitoring layer and main source for individualization/repeatability; deduplicated against A by cohort and reported separately with overlap.
- Background-only studies and excluded source types remain outside core denominators. Public preprints, if found in defined supplemental sources, are registered separately and do not enter peer-reviewed core counts. Neither the term “open window” nor an author's stance on it affects eligibility.

2.4 **Information sources and search strategy.** Name the actually searched platforms, exact platform/interface for each database, dates, coverage, search strings, limits, and supplementary citation-chasing. Planned sources are PubMed (reported as PubMed, not as a separate MEDLINE-platform search), Web of Science Core Collection, Scopus, Embase (platform to be finalized), and SPORTDiscus/EBSCOhost. Explain that the search is **construct-based**: two routes (exercise + immune terms; exercise + omics terms) with union/deduplication, and the 0–72 h organising window and all eligibility rules applied at screening, because original studies rarely use the phrase (18 PubMed title/abstract records combining “open window”, exercise and immune terms, 2018–2026, in the 4 October 2026 re-check). “Open window”-type terms appear only as OR supplements or for citation chasing, never as AND terms; “precision” and “biomarker” are not required terms either. Window-based charting has precedent in prespecified recovery windows (Trybulski et al. 2026, *Int J Mol Sci* 27(11):4950, doi:10.3390/ijms27114950, PMID 42278474; verified against PubMed esummary/efetch 4 October 2026, abstract: "Sampling was mapped to prespecified recovery windows"). Disclose the pre-archive work: the internal protocol (January 2026), the 2 October 2026 public-web reconnaissance (Codex) and the **4 October 2026 collision re-check** of the narrowed framing against published and registered reviews (Claude Code; `02_preliminary/collision_recheck_2026-10-04.md`), including the registry scan (OSF, PROSPERO, INPLASY, Research Registry, Zenodo) that will be repeated before submission together with forward citation tracking of Peake et al. 2017 and Campbell & Turner 2018. None of these is represented as formal five-database searching. Draft strategies remain unvalidated until platform testing and independent PRESS review. The final search cutoff is the actual last search date.

2.5 **Selection process.** Two human reviewers independently screen titles/abstracts and full texts in per-reviewer Excel workbooks generated from the JSON templates by `scripts/build_workbooks.py`; each reviewer locks their workbook, its SHA-256 is committed to git, and agreement/conflicts are computed by `scripts/merge_screening.py` (no Rayyan/Covidence). Report pilot sampling seed, the 50-record calibration (after the archived release), raw agreement and descriptive kappa, conceptual disagreements, and whether a repeat pilot was needed. Persistent conflicts follow the designated adjudication process after the adjudicator is named. Report one primary full-text exclusion reason (FT01–FT08) while preserving other notes; there is no window- or intensity-based exclusion code. Give actual report-selection flow counts only after screening.

2.6 **Data charting.** State that the 10 purposive boundary/design reports (including an adolescent cohort used as a population boundary case) are calibration cases, not an included-study count. Describe independent human extraction of eligibility, timing and window position, exercise duration/intensity as reported, age range and adult-stratum separability, marker family, n, effects/uncertainty, author open-window interpretation (descriptive), precision domains, function/clinical outcomes, validation, and appraisal; bibliographic metadata entry with second-person verification; Excel extraction workbooks generated from the v3 data dictionary with CSV exports versioned in git. Define `cohort_id`, `study_id`, `report_id`, and assay-level observation. Distinguish participants, arms, biospecimens, cells/preparations, assays, and technical repeats; retain same-participant pairing.

2.7 **Critical appraisal.** Name the prespecified design-appropriate tools used and the reporting date/version. Summarize domains descriptively; no aggregate quality grade, no GRADE, and no unstated appraisal-based eligibility threshold.

2.8 **Synthesis.** Describe cohort-first and report-second counting; A/B overlap; domain-specific denominators; NR/NA/UNCLEAR; handling of missingness, nulls, inconsistent findings, and exact sampling times. State display-only time bins, within-window versus outside-window recovery presentation, and separate exercise timing from symptom timing. Explain the distinction between function co-measured and marker-function association, between pathway annotation and direct function, and between internal and independent validation. Domain evidence is presented only as the validation-readiness map defined above: no meta-analysis, clinical ranking, or composite precision-readiness score.

### 3. Results

Populate only from locked search/screen/charting records. Recommended order:

3.1 **Selection of sources.** PRISMA-ScR flow diagram (supplementary), with real database/other-source and full-text counts. Never substitute zero for “not yet available.”

3.2 **Characteristics of the evidence base.** Independent cohorts first; publication reports separately. Summarize population (including the older-adult subgroup), design, exercise (mode, duration, intensity as reported), A/B strata, matrices/platforms, marker families, and immune-link basis. Flag overlapping reports/cohorts and validation cohorts.

3.3 **RQ1 — Marker families and platform landscape within the window.** Conventional markers, immune-cell omics, biofluid omics with a stated immune objective, matrix, exercise/population context, and stated monitoring use. Describe breadth without ranking by assay or number of analytes.

3.4 **RQ2 — Timing, recovery, confounding and author interpretation.** Show exact source-reported timing with display bins, within-window coverage separately from outside-window recovery; compare cell, mediator, pathway, functional, and clinical layers. Map control of hydration, nutrition/fasting, training background, plasma-volume changes, sample handling, and other confounders. Report the author open-window interpretation tags descriptively. Do not interpret non-significance as recovery.

3.5 **RQ3 — Person-level evidence.** Summarize individual baselines, within-person variance, repeatability (especially Support B), subgroup analyses, response heterogeneity, model development, participant separation, independent validation, and whether a stated monitoring or decision use was actually tested. Distinguish group-average effects from reproducible individual differences.

3.6 **RQ4 — Function, clinical outcomes, prediction and the validation-readiness map.** Separate function measured in the same cohort from a tested marker-function association; separate pathway inference from direct functional assays; separate self-reported symptoms, clinician-defined syndrome, and pathogen-confirmed infection. Summarize predictive model performance, independent validation, and tested monitoring/decision use as separate states; present the per-marker validation-readiness map (Figure 2); do not infer tested use from a proposed application or imply deployment.

3.7 **Critical appraisal.** Domain-wise descriptive results and limitations by design; no aggregate grades.

3.8 **Frontier evidence.** A separate, clearly labeled preprint table/register and separate denominators. State version and publication status at the final update.

### 4. Discussion

4.1 **Principal mapped findings**, organized by RQ1–RQ4 and limited to the evidence actually charted.

4.2 **The open window as organising construct: timing and immune compartments.** Discuss what the map shows about marker kinetics, leukocyte redistribution, immune function, and clinical infection within and beyond the organising window, and why sampling timing and control conditions matter, without adjudicating whether an open window exists. Relate the findings to the Peake et al. (2017) starting point.

4.3 **Precision-monitoring implications.** Discuss the seven-domain profile (analytical reliability, temporal validity, immune specificity, individualization, functional/clinical linkage, independent validation and tested use, feasibility) as shown in the validation-readiness map. Identify which links are absent or NR; do not rank clinical candidates or claim readiness in any scored sense (validation-readiness map definition above).

4.4 **Functional and infection linkage.** Explain what co-measurement can establish versus explicit association; assess whether functional assays, prospective follow-up, case definitions, and prediction validation support intended use.

4.5 **Research priorities.** Propose repeatable within-person sampling across bouts, defined exercise exposure and timing within the window, cell-composition/volume/nutrition controls, fit-for-purpose assay reliability, prespecified subgroups (including older adults), test-retest estimates, independent validation, and clinically meaningful outcome ascertainment where justified by the map.

4.6 **Strengths and limitations.** Discuss search coverage and platform variations, construct-based searching, language/access limits, the adult-only and acute/repeated-bout scope, study heterogeneity, missing reporting, design-appropriate appraisal, and the risk of overinterpreting high-dimensional or group-average evidence. Clearly disclose the preliminary web reconnaissance, the 4 October 2026 collision re-check, and any protocol amendments or re-releases.

4.7 **Conclusion.** State what the map can and cannot support. Do not conclude that an open window is proven or disproven (the review does not adjudicate it), that a marker predicts infection unless validated evidence supports it, or that the review establishes a clinical decision rule.
