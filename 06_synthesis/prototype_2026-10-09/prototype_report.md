## (a) Purpose and data provenance

A asked to see now what the final tables and figures of the review would look like, and whether they can be built at all. This folder builds every planned output it can from the data that already exist. It uses four read-only sources.

- **Deep layer:** 130 full-text reports extracted by the AI (PRE-009 pre-fill, `05_extraction/ai_extraction/runs/sol_formal/`, git-ignored). The B-side full-text pre-fill (PRE-010) gives 85 INCLUDE_* and 45 AWAITING_CLASSIFICATION, which is mostly the age rule. The C-side pre-fill on the same 130 gives 94 INCLUDE_*, 27 AWAITING, 8 EXCLUDE and 1 RETAIN_BACKGROUND. Both sides say INCLUDE for 76 reports.
- **V layer, abstract level:** 577 V records in `final_triage_4298.csv`.
- **Map layer:** 3,027 M records in `map_layer_abstract_level.csv`.

The 130 reports are the open-access part of the V layer: 141 of 578 V records were retrieved, and 141 minus 7 EXCLUDE minus 4 RETAIN_BACKGROUND leaves 130. Everything is regenerated by `build_prototype.py`. Run `--help` for options, and use `--status-filter include` to keep only reports with an INCLUDE pre-fill. Unless stated otherwise, the counts cover all 130 reports.

**Build log.** The script runs in about 1 second. The whole build, from first reading the sources to finishing this report, took about 15 minutes of agent wall-clock on 2026-10-09. Things I could not do are listed under "NOT YET POSSIBLE" and in section (c).

**How the evidence states were mapped.** The extraction already uses the spec's six `evidence_state` codes and its seven `precision_domain` ids, so no recoding was needed. The crosswalk-consistent subset in Figure 2B and the `evidence_present_strict` columns of T3 is a documented sensitivity view, not a recode. An `evidence_present` row is dropped from it when:

- its own note says nothing was reported or tested (detected by a regex); or
- in `independent_validation_and_use`, `validation_split` is not `independent_site_or_cohort` and the subtype is not independent replication or field use; or
- in `functional_clinical_linkage`, `linkage_subtype` is `no_linkage_evidence`, NA or NR.

**Helpers used only for display.** Analyte names are normalised (IL-x, CRP, sIgA, leukocyte counts and case variants). Matrix and assay are put into classes by keyword. Abstract-level families use the existing keyword rules in `scripts/build_map_layer.py`.

## (c) Feasibility assessment for A, in plain language

**Short answer: the review is achievable, and the output tables come out of the data almost automatically.** The real work and the real risk sit in two places: human checking of the precision-domain states, and harmonising a handful of free-text fields. The heavy process work so far did produce a dataset that maps onto every planned output except cohort deduplication and appraisal.

**What the research questions will look like with about 350–450 confirmed V-layer reports.** That is roughly three times the current 130, but the current set is the open-access part and is newer than the V layer as a whole. 128 of the 130 reports are from 2010 or later, against 435 of 577 V records (75%). It also over-represents omics: 55 of 130 deep reports (42%) contain an omics measurement, while each omics family appears in only 1–12% of V abstracts. Scaling these counts up therefore flatters omics.

- **RQ1 (landscape): fully answerable, and rich.** Cytokines and inflammatory mediators are measured in 82 of 130 reports and leukocyte redistribution in 41. Every omics family has at least one report: proteome 22, metabolome/lipidome 19. Family profiles agree well across the M, V and deep layers (T7, F4). There are 1,109 distinct analytes.
- **RQ2 (timing): answerable, and the result is clear-cut.** Within the window, coverage falls off steeply with time. The 0 to <30 min bin has 96 cohorts, 30 min to <3 h has 54, 3 to <24 h has 29 and 24 to 72 h has 42. Beyond 72 h there are only 12. Very few cohorts per family have two or more within-window bins, which is what kinetics need: 35 cohorts for cytokines, 20 for leukocytes, and 9–11 per omics family. The author open-window tag is answerable too: 93 of 130 reports give no window interpretation, and only 12 use the term explicitly.
- **RQ3 (individualization, repeatability): answerable but thin.** Individualization is `evidence_present` in 34 reports, or 22 in the strict view. Support B, repeated bouts, gives 52 reports and 57 cohorts, so it is a real evidence source and worth keeping.
- **RQ4 (function, clinical, validation, use): the answer will mostly be "absent", and that is the finding.** Strictly, functional or clinical linkage is present in 23 of 130 reports. Independent validation or tested use shows up in only 4 cohorts from 2 reports, FS-006018 and FS-006687, and the C-side pre-fill says EXCLUDE for both. Expect about 0–10 such reports in the final set.

**Thin or empty outputs.**

- Per-analyte Figure 2: only 20 analytes appear in five or more reports (IL-6 32, CRP 30, leukocyte count 25, TNF-alpha 19, then IL-10, the lymphocyte, neutrophil and monocyte counts, IL-8, sIgA and others).
- Independent-validation column: about 0–2% of reports.
- Older adults: 2 cohorts.
- Outside-window (>72 h) recovery: 12 cohorts.
- Epigenome and single-cell: 1 report each.
- Effect-size charting: `effect_value` is NR in 30% of rows and bare-numeric in only 36%; `effect_uncertainty` is NR in 57%.

**Where the AI extraction is weak.** All counts are in `T8_extraction_quality.csv` and `F5_evidence_state_diagnostic.png`.

1. **Evidence states are semantically unreliable. This is the biggest risk.**
   - NR is almost never used: 42 of 1,868 rows. The AI writes `not_demonstrated` where nothing was reported, which is 1,009 rows.
   - It also writes `evidence_present` with notes that say the opposite. That happens in 260 of 801 such rows, including 112 of 204 in feasibility (example: "No cost, turnaround time … reported"). A 25-row spot check found about 19 genuine contradictions; the rest were caveats on evidence that was otherwise present.
   - In 9 of the 13 independent-validation `evidence_present` rows, `validation_split` is not independent.
   - As a result, the feasibility and temporal-validity columns of Figure 2A are inflated. The 2B view cuts feasibility from 95 to 53 reports and analytical reliability from 84 to 66.
2. **Vocabulary drift in fields the dictionary controls but the schema left free.** `matrix` has 107 variants (84% outside the vocabulary), `exercise_mode` 139 (95%), `design` (100%), `training_status` (89%) and `clinical_endpoint` 152 variants. `time_unit` also appears as "minutes", "hours", "days" and similar. Keyword mapping rescues matrix and assay; exercise-mode strata need either a schema fix or a recode pass.
3. **Analyte names.** There are 1,219 raw names; light normalisation brings this to 1,109. A proper synonym table (HGNC, miRBase, UniProt) is needed before any per-marker map.
4. **Numeric time.** Of the 884 post-exercise sample sets, 576 have a parseable numeric time, and 570 of those (99%) agree with the AI's bin. The other 308 are NR (258) or ranges such as "0–5". So time bins are reliable, but exact times are often missing.
5. **What works well.** IDs, links, time bins, marker family (no drift at all), result_direction, record_type and validation_split all follow their vocabularies. Every row has a page-cited quote, which makes human checking fast.

**What to simplify or drop.**

- Build Figure 2 at family level only, conventional vs omics, and move per-analyte rows to a supplementary table limited to analytes in three or more reports.
- Collapse the six evidence states to three for display: reported-present, reported-null-or-not-demonstrated, and not reported. Keep the six-state codes in the data.
- Drop the feasibility domain from the headline figure unless humans re-code it, or show it as "reported or not reported" only.
- Drop effect-size charting. Direction and significance are enough for a scoping review.
- Treat the older-adult subgroup and the >72 h recovery as one-line findings, not strata.

**The single most valuable result.** A family-level validation-readiness map showing that post-exercise immune markers are richly measured and well timed in the first 3 hours, sparsely after 24 hours, and almost never individually validated, independently replicated or tested for use. A short, verifiable list of the 6–15 analytes with repeat-bout or individualization evidence (IL-6, CRP, sIgA, the leukocyte, lymphocyte and neutrophil counts, and others) completes it. This "measured everywhere, validated nowhere" gap, with numbers per domain, is the publishable contribution, and the current data already support its shape.
