Novelty lane — evidence checked to 2026-10-02

The earlier collision report needs a substantial qualification. A close direct neighbor exists: Teodoro et al. (2025) explicitly asks whether post-exercise immune alteration in older adults can characterize an “open window,” and systematically reviews acute and chronic Th1/Th2 markers across 14 studies and 525 participants. It is narrow—older adults and a single immune axis—and does not assess infection incidence, omics integration, or biomarker prediction. The defensible distinction is therefore the proposed review’s breadth across human exercise settings and biomarker layers, plus separate mapping of function, infection outcomes, and validation. Do not claim no review has ever used an open-window frame.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11850391/

Other nearest neighbors set clear boundaries:

- Kotewitsch et al. (2024) is a systematic review of exercise-regulated ncRNAs in healthy humans (95 included studies; miRNA/lncRNA/circRNA focus). It separates acute/chronic and endurance/resistance findings, but does not integrate conventional marker panels or direct infection outcomes. Its “validated” categories mean repeated directional consistency, not clinical validation or infection prediction. PMID 37925072; DOI 10.1016/j.jshs.2023.11.001. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11116971/
- Bedrač et al. (2024) is a PRISMA-ScR review of 52 studies on precision sports nutrition, omics and wearables in endurance athletes. It establishes that “scoping review + omics + athletes/precision” is already used; its PCC is nutrition/performance/recovery, with no immune or infection focus. DOI 10.3390/nu16223943. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11597302/
- Walzik et al. (2024 issue; first online 2023) reviews acute and training-related B-cell, immunoglobulin and secretory-immunity markers in adults across 67 studies. The review notes heterogeneity and uncertainty about whether immunoglobulin shifts explain infection risk; infection itself was not the outcome set. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11116964/
- Ringleb et al. (2026) reviews 167 studies (3,622 participants) but includes only healthy adults aged 18–50 and immediate post-exercise myokines, with sampling within five minutes. This makes it a direct challenge to the protocol’s current exclusion of all samples before 30 minutes. PMID 41661185; DOI 10.1096/fj.202504780R. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12885107/
- Khanzade et al. (2026) reports different NLR behavior immediately versus 1–3 hours after acute exercise and after chronic training; it pools broad ages/health states and does not assess exercise-related infection. Its very high heterogeneity cautions against treating NLR as a validated individual marker. PROSPERO CRD420251042422. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42390909/
- The 2026 inflammatory-biomarker umbrella review covers many populations and systematic reviews, but mainly circulating CRP/cytokines and mostly chronic/disease-oriented effects; it explicitly warns against transferring chronic disease effects to acute responses in healthy people. DOI 10.3389/fimmu.2026.1892544. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2026.1892544/full
- Sardeli et al. (2024) meta-analyzes post-marathon URTI symptoms (seven studies; 18% higher reported incidence after a marathon), with no biomarker measures and no pathogen-confirmed endpoint. PROSPERO CRD42022380991. https://journals.ub.uni-giessen.de/eir/article/view/1759
- Davison et al. (2025) is an invited methodological review, not a systematic marker review. It argues that ex-vivo marker changes may fail to track URTI risk and recommends validated symptom logs, pathogen screening, in-vivo markers, larger samples and stronger control of confounding. DOI 10.14814/phy2.70479. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12290944/
- Minuzzi et al. (2026) is a narrative review on immunometabolomics, metabolites and multi-omics in exercise across tissues, time and modalities. It is highly adjacent to the omics rationale but does not map infection endpoints or the full traditional-marker-to-prediction chain. DOI 10.1146/annurev-anchem-091024-095826. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42141366/

The protocol should separate marker change, immune function, infection association, and prediction/validation. It should distinguish acute challenge from training adaptation and chart time bins rather than exclude all samples before 30 minutes; new acute reviews include immediate samples and show separate 1–3-hour signals. If “precision” remains in the title, define it by within-person repeatability, baseline adjustment, outcome-specific predictive performance and external validation. Keep healthy human evidence as the core and flag older adults as a subgroup; chart disease/animal evidence separately or exclude it consistently.

Important corrections to the earlier collision report:

1. Hacker et al., “Biomarkers in Endurance Exercise: Individualized Regulation and Predictive Value,” is an original study, not a narrative review, and it appeared in BioMed Research International in 2023 (DOI 10.1155/2023/6614990), not Translational Sports Medicine. It used 49 participants across two strenuous endurance exercise trials and predicted biomarker regulation/recovery measures; it did not validate infection-risk prediction. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11022769/
2. “Immunometabolism: A Multi-Omics Approach to Interpreting the Influence of Exercise and Diet on the Immune System” is by David C. Nieman, Mary Ann Lila and Nicholas D. Gillitt (2019), not Nieman and Wentz. Nieman and Wentz authored a separate 2019 review, “The Compelling Link Between Physical Activity and the Body’s Defense System.” https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30633566/
3. The prior report’s methods call its databases “simulated,” so its categorical “no direct or near collision/first review” conclusion is not based on a reproducible live database search. It also omitted the 2025 Th1/Th2 review above.

Targeted public-web searches and source checks were completed; no obvious correction/retraction notice surfaced, but this was not a full retraction-database audit. I did not run direct searches/export in Embase, Scopus, Web of Science or SPORTDiscus, and no formal PRISMA counts are claimed. A current PROSPERO/database search remains necessary before registration and submission.
