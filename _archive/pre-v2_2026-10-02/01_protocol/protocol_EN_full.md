## A. Background and Rationale

### A.1 The "Open Window" Hypothesis: Origins and Controversy

The "open window" hypothesis, first articulated in the early 1990s, posits that prolonged, high-intensity exercise induces a transient period of immunodepression lasting approximately 3–72 hours post-exercise, during which athletes may exhibit heightened susceptibility to opportunistic infections, particularly upper respiratory tract infections (URTIs). This conceptual framework has guided decades of research in exercise immunology and informed practical recommendations for athlete health management.

However, the validity and clinical significance of this hypothesis remain contested. Campbell and Turner (2018), in a widely cited critique, challenged key tenets of the open window theory, arguing that post-exercise lymphopenia reflects beneficial immune cell redistribution to peripheral tissues rather than functional immunosuppression. Despite this reappraisal, the debate remains unresolved, and the practical need to monitor athletes' immune status during intensive training periods persists regardless of the mechanistic interpretation.

### A.2 Evolution from Traditional Indices to Multi-Omics Approaches

Historically, exercise immunology has relied on a limited panel of traditional biomarkers to assess post-exercise immune perturbations, including:

- Circulating leukocyte and lymphocyte subset counts
- Salivary immunoglobulin A (sIgA) secretion rates
- Plasma cytokine concentrations (IL-6, IL-10, TNF-α)
- Serum cortisol levels
- Natural killer (NK) cell counts and cytolytic activity

While these markers have provided valuable insights, their predictive validity for infection risk and their sensitivity to individual variability remain limited. The advent of high-throughput omics technologies—transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics, and epigenomics—has opened new avenues for characterizing the molecular landscape of exercise-induced immune modulation with unprecedented resolution.

### A.3 Identified Research Gap

A systematic literature assessment conducted prior to this protocol confirmed that no existing review has comprehensively mapped the full spectrum of biomarkers—spanning traditional immunological indices and emerging multi-omics candidates—within the specific framework of the exercise-induced "open window." Existing reviews have either:

1. Debated the theoretical validity of the open window hypothesis without systematically cataloguing biomarker evidence;
2. Focused on single biomarker categories (e.g., sIgA, NK cells) in isolation;
3. Discussed multi-omics applications in general exercise physiology without targeting the immune vulnerability period; or
4. Addressed precision medicine concepts without operationalizing them for exercise-induced immune monitoring.

This scoping review addresses this gap by providing the first systematic landscape mapping of biomarker evidence across biological scales, evaluating their collective potential for precision athlete immune health monitoring.

## B. Objectives and Research Questions

### B.1 Primary Objective

To systematically map and synthesize the existing evidence on biomarkers—from traditional immunological indices to emerging multi-omics candidates—that have been investigated in the context of exercise-induced immune perturbation (the "open window" period), and to identify candidates with potential for precision monitoring applications.

### B.2 Research Questions

This scoping review will address the following research questions:

**RQ1.** What biomarkers have been investigated to characterize or monitor immune status during the post-exercise "open window" period, and what is the breadth of evidence for each?

**RQ2.** To what extent have multi-omics technologies (transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics, epigenomics) been applied to identify novel biomarker candidates in this context?

**RQ3.** What biological matrices (blood, saliva, urine, sweat) have been utilized for biomarker sampling, and how do biomarker profiles vary across sampling sources?

**RQ4.** Which biomarkers or biomarker panels demonstrate potential for translation into precision monitoring tools for individualized athlete immune health management?

## C. Study Design

### C.1 Rationale for Scoping Review Methodology

This study will employ a scoping review methodology following the framework outlined by Arksey and O'Malley (2005), refined by Levac et al. (2010), and operationalized according to the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) guidelines for scoping reviews. The Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) checklist will guide reporting.

A scoping review, rather than a systematic review with meta-analysis, is appropriate for this investigation because:

1. **Breadth over depth:** The primary aim is to map the extent, range, and nature of biomarker evidence across multiple biological scales, rather than to synthesize effect estimates for a narrowly defined intervention-outcome relationship.

2. **Heterogeneous evidence base:** The anticipated literature encompasses diverse study designs, biomarker types, exercise protocols, and outcome measures that preclude meaningful quantitative pooling.

3. **Concept clarification:** The review seeks to clarify how "open window biomarkers" have been conceptualized and operationalized across studies, which is a key function of scoping reviews.

4. **Knowledge gap identification:** A secondary aim is to identify gaps in the current evidence base to inform future primary research priorities.

## D. Eligibility Criteria

Eligibility criteria are structured according to the Population-Concept-Context (PCC) framework recommended by JBI for scoping reviews.

### D.1 Population

**Inclusion:**
- Human participants of any age engaging in acute bouts of exercise or structured training programs
- Healthy individuals, including recreational exercisers, trained athletes, and elite competitors
- Studies examining both sexes

**Exclusion:**
- Animal models or in vitro studies without human validation
- Clinical populations where exercise is prescribed as therapeutic intervention for a pre-existing disease (e.g., cancer rehabilitation, cardiovascular disease management)
- Studies exclusively examining sedentary populations without exercise intervention

### D.2 Concept

**Inclusion:**
- Studies investigating biomarkers of immune status, immune function, or infection susceptibility in the context of exercise
- Biomarkers at any biological level: cellular (leukocyte subsets), protein (cytokines, immunoglobulins), metabolic (metabolites), or molecular (gene expression, epigenetic marks)
- Studies explicitly or implicitly addressing the post-exercise immune perturbation period ("open window," immunodepression, immune recovery)

**Exclusion:**
- Studies focused solely on performance biomarkers (e.g., lactate, VO₂max) without immune-relevant outcomes
- Studies examining chronic disease biomarkers unrelated to acute immune function
- Studies addressing allergy or autoimmunity without relevance to infection susceptibility

### D.3 Context

**Inclusion:**
- Acute exercise protocols (single bouts of any modality)
- Training studies examining immune biomarker changes across training cycles
- Competition settings where post-exercise immune monitoring was conducted
- Any geographical or institutional setting

**Exclusion:**
- Studies examining only resting baseline immune parameters without exercise challenge
- Studies with follow-up periods less than 30 minutes post-exercise (insufficient to capture "open window" dynamics)

### D.4 Types of Sources

This scoping review will consider:

- **Primary research:** Observational studies (cross-sectional, cohort, case-control), experimental studies (randomized and non-randomized trials), and quasi-experimental designs
- **Secondary research:** Systematic reviews and narrative reviews will be examined for reference mining but will be charted separately and not conflated with primary evidence

Conference abstracts, dissertations, and preprints will be excluded unless full-text peer-reviewed versions are available.

## E. Search Strategy

### E.1 Information Sources

A comprehensive search will be conducted across the following electronic databases:

| Database | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| PubMed/MEDLINE | Primary biomedical database |
| Web of Science Core Collection | Multidisciplinary coverage, citation tracking |
| Scopus | Broad scientific coverage, European literature |
| SPORTDiscus | Specialized sports science database |
| Embase | Additional biomedical coverage |

Supplementary search strategies will include:
- Backward citation tracking of included studies and relevant reviews
- Forward citation tracking of seminal papers (e.g., Campbell & Turner, 2018; Nieman, 2020)
- Hand-searching of key journals: *Exercise Immunology Review*, *Journal of Sport and Health Science*, *British Journal of Sports Medicine*

### E.2 Search Terms and Boolean Strategy

Search terms will be developed iteratively with input from a research librarian. The strategy will combine three concept blocks using Boolean operators:

**Concept Block 1: Exercise**
```
("exercise" OR "physical activity" OR "training" OR "sport*" OR "athletic"
OR "endurance" OR "resistance training" OR "high-intensity" OR "strenuous")
```

**Concept Block 2: Immune/Open Window**
```
("immune*" OR "immunity" OR "immunosuppression" OR "immune function"
OR "open window" OR "infection susceptibility" OR "URTI"
OR "upper respiratory" OR "mucosal immunity")
```

**Concept Block 3: Biomarkers**
```
("biomarker*" OR "marker*" OR "indicator*" OR "cytokine*" OR "interleukin*"
OR "immunoglobulin*" OR "IgA" OR "leukocyte*" OR "lymphocyte*" OR "NK cell*"
OR "cortisol" OR "transcriptom*" OR "proteom*" OR "metabolom*" OR "epigenet*"
OR "omics" OR "multi-omics")
```

**Combined Strategy:**
```
(Block 1) AND (Block 2) AND (Block 3)
```

### E.3 Limits and Restrictions

- **Language:** English-language publications only (with acknowledgment of this limitation)
- **Time frame:** January 2000 to present (capturing the modern omics era while including foundational traditional biomarker studies)
- **Publication type:** Peer-reviewed journal articles

## F. Study Selection

### F.1 Screening Process

Study selection will proceed through two stages:

**Stage 1: Title and Abstract Screening**
- Two reviewers will independently screen all titles and abstracts against eligibility criteria
- A calibration exercise will be conducted on the first 50 records to ensure consistent application of criteria
- Studies meeting inclusion criteria or with uncertain eligibility will advance to full-text review

**Stage 2: Full-Text Review**
- Two reviewers will independently assess full texts of potentially eligible studies
- Reasons for exclusion will be documented at this stage

### F.2 Conflict Resolution

- Discrepancies between reviewers will be resolved through discussion
- Persistent disagreements will be adjudicated by a third reviewer
- Inter-rater reliability will be calculated and reported (Cohen's kappa or percent agreement)

### F.3 Software and Documentation

- Reference management: EndNote or Zotero
- Screening management: Rayyan or Covidence
- A PRISMA-ScR flow diagram will document the selection process

## G. Data Charting

### G.1 Data Charting Form

A standardized data charting form will be developed, pilot-tested on 10 studies, and refined iteratively. The form will capture the following domains:

**Study Characteristics:**
- Author(s), year of publication, country
- Study design
- Sample size

**Population Characteristics:**
- Participant demographics (age, sex)
- Training status (untrained, recreationally active, trained, elite)
- Sport type or exercise modality

**Exercise Protocol:**
- Type of exercise (endurance, resistance, mixed, competition)
- Intensity and duration
- Acute versus chronic/training study

**Biomarker Information:**
- Biomarker name(s) and category (traditional immune marker, cytokine, omics-derived, etc.)
- Biological matrix (blood, saliva, urine, other)
- Sampling time points relative to exercise
- Analytical method

**Key Findings:**
- Direction and magnitude of biomarker change
- Association with clinical outcomes (e.g., URTI incidence) if reported
- Authors' conclusions regarding biomarker utility

### G.2 Charting Process

- Two reviewers will independently chart data from included studies
- Discrepancies will be resolved through discussion and reference to source documents
- Authors of primary studies will not be contacted for missing data (consistent with scoping review methodology)

## H. Synthesis and Presentation of Results

### H.1 Approach to Evidence Synthesis

Consistent with scoping review methodology, this review will employ a descriptive-analytical approach rather than quantitative meta-analysis. The synthesis will characterize the nature, extent, and distribution of evidence rather than pooling effect estimates.

### H.2 Planned Outputs

**Narrative Synthesis:**
A narrative summary will address each research question, describing patterns, trends, and gaps in the evidence base.

**Tabular Presentations:**

| Table Type | Content |
|------------|---------|
| Summary of included studies | Author, year, design, population, exercise protocol, biomarkers assessed |
| Biomarker inventory | Comprehensive list of all biomarkers identified, categorized by biological level |
| Matrix-biomarker correspondence | Mapping of biomarkers to sampling matrices |
| Evidence density map | Number of studies per biomarker category |

**Visual Presentations:**

1. **Biomarker-Omics Hierarchical Map:** A conceptual diagram illustrating the landscape of biomarkers from traditional indices (cellular, hormonal) to multi-omics candidates (transcriptomic, proteomic, metabolomic, epigenomic)

2. **Temporal Evidence Map:** Timeline visualization showing the evolution of biomarker research in this field, highlighting the transition from traditional to omics-based approaches

3. **Gap Analysis Matrix:** Visual representation of well-characterized versus under-investigated biomarker-context combinations

### H.3 Addressing Research Questions

Each research question will be addressed through dedicated synthesis subsections, with findings triangulated across tabular, visual, and narrative formats.

## I. Critical Appraisal of Evidence Sources

### I.1 Position Statement

Consistent with established scoping review methodology (JBI, 2020; PRISMA-ScR), formal critical appraisal of methodological quality or risk of bias in individual studies is **not mandatory** and will **not be conducted** in this review.

### I.2 Methodological Justification

This decision is grounded in the following rationale:

1. **Scoping review purpose:** The primary aim is to map the extent and nature of evidence, not to synthesize effect estimates where study quality directly influences pooled conclusions.

2. **Anticipated heterogeneity:** The included studies will span diverse designs, outcomes, and contexts, rendering standardized quality assessment scales of limited interpretive value.

3. **JBI guidance alignment:** The JBI Manual for Evidence Synthesis explicitly states that "scoping reviews do not aim to produce a critically appraised and synthesized result/answer to a particular question" and that quality assessment "is generally not performed."

### I.3 Alternative Quality Considerations

While formal appraisal is not conducted, the following quality-relevant information will be extracted and reported descriptively:

- Study design classification
- Sample size
- Use of validated biomarker assays
- Appropriateness of sampling time points

This information will contextualize findings and inform recommendations for future research.
